# Seafaring (`seafaring`) as implemented

The `seafaring` module depends on `base` and is compatible with `five-six`. Everything not listed here follows [the base rules](base.md), and with `five-six` also [the five-six rules](five-six.md).

Sources, all read on 2026-09-28:

- **Rulebook (2021)**: the publisher's [Seafarers rules and scenarios](https://www.catan.com/sites/default/files/2021-06/catan-seafarers_2021_rule_book_201201.pdf), fifth English edition ("as of December 1, 2020, all of the rules in this version … take precedence"). Cited as _2021_.
- **Rulebook (2025)**: the publisher's [Seafarers rulebook, v6.250401](https://www.catan.com/sites/default/files/2025-03/CN3083%20CATAN%E2%80%93Seafarers%20Rulebook%202025%20secured%20reduced.pdf). Cited as _2025_. Where the two differ, _2025_ is the newer text and wins.
- **FAQ**: the publisher's [Seafarers FAQ](https://www.catan.com/faq/seafarers). Cited as _FAQ_.
- **5–6 extension**: the [2015 Seafarers 5–6 extension rules](https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/fa/50/04-catan-seafarers-5-6-player-extension-rulebook.pdf) (special build phase) and the [2025 Seafarers 5–6 rulebook](https://www.catan.com/sites/default/files/2025-03/CN3084%20CATAN%20%E2%80%93%20Seafarers_%205-6%20Player_%20Rulebook.pdf) (paired players, not implemented, see [five-six.md](five-six.md)).

Nothing in the publisher's texts covers bank shortage for gold fields, gold in setup, gold or desert on discovery, several fog hexes revealed by one placement, fog and islands changing shape, or a bank that cannot pay a discovery reward. Those items are marked "(our choice)". The publisher's scenario maps are never reproduced (see Scenarios).

## Board

- Terrains add `sea`, `gold` and `fog`. `desert` stays a land terrain. A `fog` hex is unrevealed and is neither land nor sea until revealed.
- **Off-board is sea.** An edge on the board perimeter has the off-board side counted as sea. So a perimeter edge of a land hex is coastal, and a perimeter edge of a sea hex is a sea edge. This is the equivalent of the rulebook's "You may always build on the inner edge of the frame but never on the outer edge" (FAQ). No edge exists outside the perimeter.
- **Land hex**: a revealed hex of any terrain other than `sea` and `fog`, gold and desert included. **Coastal edge**: land on one side and sea on the other. **Sea edge**: sea on both sides. **Land edge**: land on both sides.
- **Land vertex**: a vertex touching at least one revealed land hex. Settlements and cities are built only on land vertices.
- **Island**: a connected component of land hexes joined by shared edges. It is computed at genesis and again after every fog reveal. Two hexes that touch at a vertex only cannot exist, because the third hex at that vertex touches both.
- **Region**: the unit of the new-island bonus. By default a region is an island. A scenario may instead declare explicit regions (sets of hexes), used by Desert Crossing.
- Harbors sit on coastal edges (perimeter edges included) and behave as in the base game: a seat trades at a harbor it holds a settlement or city on, at either end of the harbor's edge. A ship next to a harbor gives nothing (only a scenario-specific rule in the publisher's book does that, and we don't implement it). No two harbors share a vertex. Scenarios fix the harbor slots and either fix the harbor kinds or shuffle them over the slots.
- Gold is not a resource. There are no gold cards. The bank and hands are unchanged: 19 of each resource, or 24 with `five-six`.

### Moving a ship (`MOVE_SHIP { from, to }`)

Once per turn, in the `main` phase only, the active seat may move one ship at no cost. It is not a build action, so it is available between builds and trades. It is not available in `preRoll`, in the road-building phase, or in a special build phase.

## The robber and the pirate

- **Robber**: a land hex only. The desert, a gold hex and an island hex are legal. A fog hex and a sea hex are not. It must move to a different hex. It starts on the desert with the lowest hex id. If the scenario has no desert, it starts off the board and enters at the first move (FAQ: "the robber stands beside the game board").
- **Pirate**: a sea hex only, and a different hex from where it is. It starts on the scenario's pirate hex, or off the board if the scenario says so. Moving it to the frame, as the 2025 text allows, is not implemented (our choice: our board has no frame hexes). A revealed sea hex is a legal destination; an unrevealed fog hex is not.
- **On a 7**, after discards, or **on playing a knight**, the active seat moves either the robber or the pirate, and never both. 2021: "you may elect to move the pirate INSTEAD of the robber". "If you play a knight card, you now have the choice of moving either the robber or the pirate." The command is chosen by the engine implementer.
- **Stealing from the robber**: as in base, from an opponent with a settlement or city on the destination hex and at least one card.
- **Stealing from the pirate**: from an opponent with **a ship on an edge of the pirate's hex** and at least one card. One card in all, however many ships that opponent has there. Owners of a coastal settlement are not eligible (FAQ: "No"). With no eligible opponent, no steal happens. A choice among several victims works as in base.
- **Blocking**: the pirate blocks new ships on its hex's edges and moves of ships from or to them. It does not block roads, settlements, cities, production or trade. The robber blocks production of its hex as in base. It does not block ships.
- **`friendlyRobber`** applies to the pirate too (our choice, for symmetry): destinations next to ships of a seat with at most two public points are removed, unless that leaves no destination, in which case every other sea hex is legal.
- Timeout: the pirate or robber choice on timeout is the engine implementer's, following the base timeout rule (first legal hex not touching the active seat when possible).

## Gold fields

- A settlement on a vertex of a gold hex whose number is rolled entitles its owner to 1 card of the owner's choice, and a city to 2 cards, in any combination, including 2 of one kind (FAQ: "It is up to you whether you want to take two resources of the same type or of different types.").
- A seat with several gold entitlements from one roll (two adjacent gold hexes with the same number, or several buildings) has a single total claim.
- The robber on the gold hex blocks it.
- Ordinary production resolves first with the base shortage rule. Gold claims then resolve in a `goldChoice` pending with `CHOOSE_GOLD { resources }`:
  - One seat at a time, in turn order starting with the active seat (our choice).
  - A seat with no gold claim is skipped. The seat's claim is capped at the bank's total stock at that moment. If the bank holds nothing, the seat gets nothing and no pending opens.
  - The seat must choose exactly `min(claim, bank total)` cards, all of kinds the bank still holds and in amounts it can pay.
  - The base "none receive it" shortage rule does not apply, because the resource is chosen and a seat can always adapt.
  - The active seat cannot start `main` until every gold pending has resolved. No trading or building happens in between.
- Reason for the sequential order: it makes bank limits deterministic and independent of message arrival, and it matches how hand-size and robber steps already run. A later implementation may open all pendings at once if it applies the choices in this same order.
- **A gold hex is a terrain hex.** It counts for islands and regions, and settling an island of gold only still earns the island bonus (FAQ: "This rule also applies if a foreign island consists only of gold fields.").
- **No gold at setup** (our choice). The second setup settlement takes 1 card per adjacent resource-producing hex, and gold hexes give nothing. The publishers' texts say nothing, and this avoids a choice pending inside setup.
- Gold cannot be traded, held or discarded.

## Islands and the new-island bonus

Scenario option `newIslandBonus: { vp: 1 | 2 }`, or absent.

- A seat's **home regions** are the regions containing its setup settlements: one or two. In fixed-start scenarios all seats share one home region.
- The **first** settlement a seat builds on each **other** region earns the bonus (a token, scoring `vp`). Later settlements on the region, and cities, earn nothing. The bonus is per seat and per region.
- Other players' settlements on the region do not matter, including on their home region. 2021 (The Four Islands): "it does not matter if other players have already built settlements on the island, or even if it is that player's home island!"
- The bonus is a permanent token attached to the settlement. It stays when the settlement becomes a city, and it counts as public points.
- Bonus tokens are public, like the settlement.
- Fog and the island bonus do not appear together in a scenario (our choice). A reveal changes island shapes, which would change which settlements have earned what, and the publisher's Fog Islands scenario has no special points ("There are no special victory point chits in this scenario"). Genesis rejects a scenario with both.
- Victory target: scenario-defined, checked as in base (a seat wins on its own turn). The FAQ formula for custom maps, "Number of terrain hexes (not counting deserts) divided by 2", is only guidance for scenario design.

## Longest Trade Route

- It replaces Longest Road. One award, worth 2 public points, needing at least 5 segments, exactly as Longest Road. 2021: "in all ways identical to the longest road". The segments are the seat's roads and ships.
- The route is the longest trail: a path may revisit vertices but cannot reuse an edge. It cannot pass through an opponent's building. A trail may end at one.
- A path switches between road and ship only at a vertex holding **the seat's own settlement or city**. Road to road and ship to ship pass through any vertex not holding an opponent building. 2021: "you can only connect a road to a shipping route if there is a settlement or a city at the intersection where the two meet." FAQ: "unless they are connected via a settlement, you still have two separate trade routes."
- Only the single longest branch counts. Closed and open shipping routes both count.
- After a ship moves or a building is placed, the award is recomputed with the base holder rules (a tie keeps it).

## Fog and exploration

- **Trigger**: when a road or ship is placed, or a ship moved, every fog hex that touches **either endpoint vertex of the edge** is revealed. That covers the two hexes on the edge's sides and the up to two hexes at its ends. 2025: "When you place a ship or road adjacent to an intersection with an empty hex space, you discover a new location." 2021: "If you place a ship or road that connects to the intersection of an unexplored hex, you must immediately draw". Settlements and cities do not reveal. Setup pieces and Road Building pieces do.
- **Order**: several hexes revealed by one placement are revealed in ascending hex id order (our choice, the publisher's texts are silent). Each reveal draws separately.
- **Draw**: the top tile of the scenario's hidden fog stacks, using the deck protocol in its public-reveal mode: every seat, the drawer included, removes its lock layer, so the tile becomes public. Terrain and token stacks are separate, as in the publisher's setup (terrain hexes and number discs are separate face-down stacks). Decks: `fog-terrain` (card types are terrains) and `fog-token` (card types are numbers as strings), both declared through the `decks` hook from `options.fog`, with zero counts left out.
- **Sequencing**: a hex takes one or two draws, and the hex itself changes only when both are done. The terrain is drawn first. `sea` and `desert` complete the hex at once. Any other terrain, gold included, then draws a token from `fog-token`, and only that draw completes the hex. The drawn terrain waits in `ext.seafaring.fog.terrain`, so a hex is never a terrain without its token. Each hex finishes, with its reward, before the next hex of the same placement starts.
- **Frame**: after the placing command has finished changing the phase stack, the `afterInput` hook pushes a `fogReveal` frame above whatever phase it left (main, a setup step, Road Building, a special build phase, even the first turn's `preRoll`). Its pending request is one public draw, plus the active seat's hidden victory claim as in every interrupt, so nobody can build, trade or end the turn until every hex is drawn. Closing the frame resumes the interrupted phase unchanged. The frame is the only place a draw happens, which keeps automatic inputs such as the special build phase's auto-end from misfiring.
- **Request and answer**: the request is `{ type: 'draw', deck, public: true, seat, slotId, remaining, hex }`, with `slotId` `${deck}:${draws so far}` and `seat` the revealer. The answer is the system input `FOG_REVEALED { deck, seat, slotId, remaining, hex, card }`, built by `publicDrawInput`. The handler checks every echoed field against the head of the queue and the card against the declared stack, records the draw and emits `deck-card-shown`. On completion it emits a `fogRevealed` event `{ seat, hex, terrain, token }`.
- **Token rule**: gold and every resource hex take a token. The token stack holds one token per such hex in the terrain stack. Red-number adjacency is not enforced in fog (2021 Fog Islands variable set-up allows it).
- **The seed does not determine fog.** Genesis fixes only how many tiles of each kind are in the stacks.
- **After a reveal**, the hex is a normal hex, island detection reruns (islands are derived from the hex list, so the next read sees the new shape), and the reveal is public.
- **Reward (land hex)**: the revealer receives 1 card of the resource the hex produces, from the bank, as soon as the hex is complete. Gold: 1 card of the revealer's choice, through a one-card `goldChoice` frame (our choice: gold produces "any resource", so this is the consistent reading). When the hex is the last of the placement, the gold frame sits directly above the interrupted phase. Otherwise it sits above the fog frame and the next draw waits for it. Desert and sea: nothing. If the bank has none of a produced resource, the revealer gets nothing (our choice: the base rule gives a sole claimant the remainder, which is zero). If the bank is empty, a gold reveal pays nothing either and opens no choice.
- **Reveal in a special build phase or Road Building**: allowed, with its reward.
- **Edge legality next to fog (our choice)**: a fog hex is never counted as land or sea. So a ship needs a revealed sea side (or off-board), and a road a revealed land side. This keeps the invariant "ships only on sea or coastal edges, roads never on sea edges" true after any reveal. A ship on a fog-and-sea edge is allowed, since it is sea-sea or sea-land after the reveal. A ship on a fog-and-land or fog-and-fog edge is not, since the reveal could make it land-land. The publishers' texts do not say.
- **Legal setup areas** never include unrevealed fog.

## Setup

- Setup settlements and roads must be inside the scenario's setup areas (the rulebook's "must be placed on the main island", or "one island or two different islands").
- A setup settlement on the coast lets the seat place a ship instead of the road (`PLACE_SETUP_SHIP { edge }`, an alternative to `PLACE_ROAD`). The ship must touch the settlement just placed, satisfy the ship edge rules, and not touch the pirate's hex. A road must touch the settlement and satisfy the road edge rules.
- Setup order, starting resources and reversal are as in base, except that gold gives nothing.
- Home regions are recorded from the setup settlements.
- With `five-six` the robber starts on the lowest-id desert.

## Development cards

- **Knight**: moves the robber or the pirate (see above). Largest Army is unchanged.
- **Road Building**: 2 free pieces, each a road or a ship. The phase ends when no legal piece remains, and a seat may skip either placement, as in base.
- Year of Plenty, Monopoly and victory-point cards are unchanged. Gold is never a legal choice.

## Special build phase (with `five-six`)

- A seat in a special build phase may also use `BUILD_SHIP`. It may not use `MOVE_SHIP`. Fog reveals (with their reward) and the pirate's block apply.
- Nobody can win during a special build phase, as in [five-six.md](five-six.md). The automatic end-of-phase convenience counts a ship as an affordable build.

### Layouts as implemented

Boards are drawn on an offset grid (odd rows sit half a hex right) and list every hex, sea included. Terrains, tokens and harbor kinds are fixed, not shuffled. Token numbers were solved once per board with no adjacent 6/8 and no adjacent equal numbers, and each terrain's average pips are kept close. Every board has one harbor per resource and the rest generic (four generic on the nine-harbor boards, five on the ten-harbor ones), all on coastal edges and none sharing a vertex. Setup areas, pirate starts and regions are hex ids in the scenario's `seafaring` option object. The bonus counts the first settlement per region, as in Islands and the new-island bonus.

**New Horizons (3–4 players, 9×7, 63 hexes).** A 21-hex home island fills the middle: four hexes of each resource and a desert at its heart, where the robber starts. Around it lie six small islands of one to three hexes (14 hexes in all, three of them gold). Setup is on the home island only, and each small island is a region worth 2 points. The pirate starts in open water off the north-west corner. Nine harbors: 8 on the home coast and 1 generic on the north-east islet. Target 14.

**New Horizons (5–6 players, 11×9, 99 hexes).** A 32-hex home island with an even mix of resources and a desert at its heart, and eight outer islands of one to three hexes (17 hexes, four gold), spread round the corners and sides. Setup on the home island only, 2 points per outer island, pirate off the north-west corner. Ten harbors: 8 on the home coast plus a generic on each of the west and east islands. Target 16.

**Four Isles (3–4 players, 9×7, 63 hexes).** Four seven-hex flowers, one in each quarter of the frame, one sea hex apart, so ships cross a one-hex channel. Two islands carry a desert on the rim (the robber starts on the north-west one) and two carry a gold field; resources are 5 forest, 5 pasture, 5 fields, 5 hills, 4 mountains. Every land hex is a setup area, so a seat may start on one island or two. The four islands are the regions, worth 2 points each for a seat's first settlement there (home islands excepted). The pirate starts at the crossing of the channels. Nine harbors, two or three per island. Target 13.

**Four Isles (5–6 players, 11×7, 77 hexes).** The same quarters with ten-hex islands (rows of 3, 4 and 3), two deserts and four gold fields. Ten harbors. Target 13.

**Fogbound (3–4 players, 9×7, 63 hexes).** Two 11-hex islands (west and east) stand either side of a vertical band of 13 fog hexes. A hex of open water separates each coast from the fog, so nothing is revealed until a ship goes out. West holds the desert and the robber. Each island has 2 or 3 of each resource. The stack holds 13 tiles: forest 2, hills 2, pasture 1, fields 2, mountains 1, gold 2 and sea 3, with ten tokens (3, 4, 5, 5, 6, 8, 9, 9, 10, 11; sea takes none). There is no island bonus (see Islands). The pirate starts on the south rim beside the fog. Both islands are setup areas. Nine harbors, split between the coasts. Target 12.

**Desert Crossing (3–4 players, 9×7, 63 hexes).** A 31-hex island whose middle is a zigzag strip of five desert hexes (robber start at its centre). West is home (13 hexes, 4 forest, 2 pasture, 3 fields, 2 hills, 2 mountains) and the setup area. East (13 hexes) has two gold fields. Four two-hex islets sit in the corners, one of them with a gold field. The desert strip joins the two sides into one connected island, so the scenario declares six explicit regions: west (home), east, and each corner islet. The strip hexes belong to no region, and the two sides never touch, so every vertex maps to at most one region. Each region other than home is worth 2 points. The pirate starts on the south rim. Nine harbors, three on the west coast, four on the east, one on each of the north-west and south-east islets. Target 13.

**Open Sea (3–4 players and 5–6 players, generated).** The board comes from `generateArchipelago` at genesis, so it is a pure function of the genesis seed. 3–4 players get a 9×7 frame: a home island of 14 to 18 hexes kept off the frame's rim, four or five islands of two to four hexes that never touch each other or the home island, three gold fields (never on the home island, at most half of an island) and nine harbors. 5–6 players get 11×9: a home island of 24 to 30 hexes, five to seven islands of two to five hexes, four gold fields and ten harbors. Every board has one desert on the home island (robber start), one body of connected sea so every island is reachable, no adjacent 6/8 or equal tokens, and resource pips within one pip of each other on average. The pirate starts on the sea hex furthest from land. Any island may host setup (no setup areas). 1 point per foreign island, target 12.

## Options

| Option         | Default | Implemented behavior                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------- | ------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `pirateHex`    | `null`  | The pirate's starting sea hex id, or `null` to start it off the board. Genesis rejects a hex that is not `sea`. An off-board pirate enters at the first move.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `setupAreas`   | `null`  | Hex ids whose vertices allow setup settlements, and whose edges allow setup roads. `null` allows any land vertex. Setup ships need no area. Fog never counts as land.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `islandBonus`  | `null`  | `{ vp }` with `vp` from 1 to 5. The first settlement a seat builds on each foreign region earns `vp` points. `null` means no bonus.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `bonusRegions` | `null`  | Explicit regions as groups of hex ids, disjoint. `null` makes every island a region. A region is named by its least hex id. A hex in no group belongs to no region and earns nothing.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| `fog`          | `null`  | `{ terrains: Record<terrain, count>, tokens: Record<token, count> }`, the contents of the two hidden stacks. Genesis checks that the terrain counts sum to the board's `fog` hexes, that every key is a known tile terrain or token number, and that the token counts sum to the tiles that take one. A board with `fog` hexes needs this option. Genesis rejects fog together with `islandBonus`, with its own message.                                                     |
| `layout`       | `fixed` | `fixed` builds from `config.board`. `archipelago`, with no `config.board`, generates the Open Sea board at genesis with `generateArchipelagoLayout(rng, archipelagoParamsFor(seats))`, and the pirate starts on the hex the generator names (`pirateStartHex`), whatever `pirateHex` says. Base then builds its own default board from the same random stream and the module replaces it, so `mapLayout` is left `random`. With an explicit board, `archipelago` is ignored. |
| `vpTarget`     | 10      | The base option. The scenario's target replaces the default.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

The remaining base options apply unchanged. The board is not an option: it is the explicit `config.board` (or the generated archipelago), and the module sets `mapLayout` to `standard-fixed` so base validates an explicit board. Seafaring builds a board shape from that board (`seafaring: true`), so validation checks structure and not counts: tokens follow terrain, harbors are on coastal edges, the robber stands on land or is `null`, and no piece is placed. A scenario may therefore have no desert and start the robber off the board.

## Implementation

State: `board.ships` (a list of `{ edge, seat }`, present only in seafaring games, so base states hash as before) and `ext.seafaring`: `pirateHex`, `builtThisTurn` (ship edges built, placed free or moved this turn), `shipMovedTurn`, `homeRegions` (per seat), `bonus` (tokens `{ seat, region, vertex }`) and, only in games with fog, `fog`: `null`, or the reveal in progress `{ seat, hexes, terrain }` (the revealer, the fog hexes still to draw in reveal order, and the head hex's drawn terrain while its token is drawn). Piece supply is `piecesLeft.ship` (15) and the cost table has `ship` (1 lumber, 1 wool).

| Command                     | Where                                        | Notes                                                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `BUILD_SHIP { edge }`       | `main`, and the five-six special build phase | Costs a ship. The edge must be a legal ship edge, connected as described under Ships.                                                        |
| `MOVE_SHIP { from, to }`    | `main` only, active seat, once per turn      | Free. See Moving a ship.                                                                                                                     |
| `PLACE_SETUP_SHIP { edge }` | Setup, in place of `PLACE_ROAD`              | The edge must touch the settlement just placed.                                                                                              |
| `PLACE_FREE_SHIP { edge }`  | Road Building                                | One of the two free pieces, next to `PLACE_FREE_ROAD` and `SKIP`. The phase ends when no legal piece is left, counting both roads and ships. |
| `MOVE_PIRATE { hex }`       | The robber phase (a 7 or a knight)           | A new command, so `MOVE_ROBBER` keeps its payload. `MOVE_ROBBER` still moves only the robber, and only to land.                              |
| `CHOOSE_GOLD { resources }` | The `goldChoice` phase                       | Exactly `min(claim, bank total)` cards, of kinds the bank holds.                                                                             |

The one module system input is `FOG_REVEALED`, the answer to a public fog draw (see [Fog](#fog-and-exploration)). It is resolved by the driver, never by a player: `LocalGame` with the local or browser random source, the simulation source, the protocol simulation driver, and the P2P deck ceremony online.

Longest Trade Route reuses the `longestRoad` award key, so base state and hashes are unchanged and the award still scores 2 points. Its route graph comes from the `routeGraph` hook. Island bonus points are a public `victoryPoints` contribution marked `stored`, so base folds them into `seat.publicVp` after every input. Victory checks, hidden-point claims, `friendlyRobber` and scoreboards then see them, and `computeVictoryPoints` does not add them twice.

Further implementation choices:

- **Gold** opens after ordinary production as a `goldChoice` frame above `main` with the queue of `{ seat, claim }`, in turn order from the active seat. A choice that empties the bank ends the queue. A timeout takes the first cards the bank holds, in canonical resource order (brick, lumber, wool, grain, ore). Legal commands list every choice when there are at most 60, and otherwise one greedy fill per starting resource.
- **Regions** for a vertex: the least region id among its land hexes that belong to a region. A vertex touching two explicit regions therefore counts for the lower id. Home regions are recorded in setup even when there is no bonus.
- **Routes** for closure and movement are chains of a seat's ships joined at shared vertices that hold no own building, so an own settlement in the middle of a chain splits it into two routes. A ship on a circle is movable as described in the table above, using the ship graph including the building vertex.
- **Fog frames** are queued by `afterBuild` (a road, a ship, or a moved ship) into `ext.seafaring.fog` and opened by the `afterInput` hook, which base calls after every finalized input that leaves no game result. A placement that wins the game ends it with the reveal undrawn. The `fogReveal` phase's only legal commands are the hidden victory claim. Invariants: the fog hexes on the board equal the undrawn `fog-terrain` cards (plus one while a drawn terrain waits for its token), and hexes wait exactly while a fog frame is on the stack.
- **Ship edges** need a revealed sea side: a sea hex or the off-board side of a perimeter edge. Fog is never counted, and a road needs a revealed land side.
- **Connectivity.** Base roads connect through the seat's own roads and buildings only. The `connectivity` hook now adds the seat's ships for settlements only, so a settlement at the end of a shipping route works without letting a ship connect a road.

## Test map

Trade-route fixtures cover road, settlement and ship chains, and a road meeting a ship at an empty vertex, which does not connect. Further cases:

- Open ends, closed routes, circles, and interrupted routes.
- Pirate blocking, in setup, in building and in moving.
- Gold shortage order.
- Fog reveals: several hexes at once, reveals by ship move, setup piece and free ship, the reward for each terrain, the empty-bank cases, island recomputation and the answer checks (`fog.test.ts`).
- Fog and secrets: two games with one genesis seed and different deck secrets reveal different tiles, through the real deck ceremony and the real Fogbound config (`tools/sim/src/fog-deck-secrets.test.ts`).
- A random-bot smoke run on every seafaring scenario, with public and private invariants on (`tools/sim/src/seafaring.test.ts`), and 400 Fogbound games with fog reveals, also with `five-six` and special build phases, run during development.
- A saved local game resumed later rebuilds its public-deck bookkeeping from the replayed log (`apps/web/src/session`).
