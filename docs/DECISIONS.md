## 2026-09-24: Clarify milestone A and B contracts before implementation

- Stage: 01, with corrections to the stage 02–04 specifications.
- Decision: Use strict-balance pip caps of 14 for resources on four hexes and 11 for resources on three hexes. Bound generation to 100 terrain retries after each 10,000-attempt token search. Keep ordinary random boards unconstrained and apply balance rules to balanced-random boards.
- Alternatives: A uniform cap of 12, or removing the pip limit. The original uniform cap of 11 is impossible.
- Reason: The tokens total 58 pips, exceeding the original five-resource capacity of 55. The chosen limits account for terrain counts and keep the limit of 11 for three-hex resources. A finite retry bound prevents genesis from hanging.
- Decision: Add `CLAIM_VICTORY { slotIds }` for hidden victory points. Public points win automatically. The local driver submits and records an immediate claim when the active seat's private total wins.
- Alternatives: Store private VP totals in public state, or let public `apply` inspect private state.
- Reason: Both alternatives violate public/private separation or deterministic replay. A claim reveals the winning cards through an explicit input and uses the existing protocol obligation to verify card reveals.
- Decision: Support two-player base games with unchanged rules, as stage 05 already requires. Put board generation under `modules/base/setup`, expose a separate RNG entry for external bots and simulators, and inject enumeration sampling. Clarify local draw ownership, trade-response pendings, offer invalidation, and the validation rules for timeout and seat-status inputs.
- Alternatives: Remove two-player setup, share a hidden RNG with rule handlers, or allow inputs without a defined phase contract.
- Reason: These choices reconcile the existing stage briefs while preserving their required behavior and engine dependency rules.
- Consultation: A read-only `claude -p` review of stages 00–05 confirmed the impossible pip cap and private-VP contradiction and identified the RNG layout, player-count and pending-model inconsistencies. The coordinator checked those findings against the documents. Suggestions to add new acceptance workloads or weaken runtime dependency rules were not adopted.

## 2026-09-24: Resource affordability must preserve a feasible hand

- Stage: 02 specification review, before implementation.
- Decision: Require `max[r] ≥ cost[r]` for every resource and `sum(max(min[r], cost[r])) ≤ total` for public affordability. Use the same guard for known losses, and normalize all bounds updates. Reject infeasible bounds and hidden losses greater than the hand total.
- Alternatives: Check only per-resource maxima, or also check only the total cost.
- Reason: A five-card hand with three guaranteed ore and at most two each of brick and lumber cannot spend two brick plus two lumber. Both alternatives accept that impossible spend. The chosen condition exactly characterizes whether some publicly consistent hand can pay, while preserving the need for later private-hand audits.
- Consultation: A read-only `claude -p` review independently proved the condition by intersecting the hand bounds with the cost lower bounds. It confirmed that the documented loss formulas are sound after this guard and normalization. Stage 02 tests must compare the operations against brute-force feasible hands as well as the required 10k soundness runs.

## 2026-09-24: Canonical byte tag and strict decoding

- Stage: 02.
- Decision: Reserve an exact one-key `{"$b":"..."}` object for `Uint8Array`. Encoding rejects every ordinary one-key object named `$b`, regardless of its value. Decoding accepts that exact shape only when its value is canonical unpadded base64url. `canonicalDecode` also requires re-encoding the decoded value to reproduce the input bytes exactly, rejecting duplicate keys, whitespace, unsorted keys and other noncanonical JSON forms.
- Alternatives: Allow ordinary objects to collide with the byte tag, or accept noncanonical JSON and normalize it during decoding.
- Reason: A single byte tag shape makes snapshot decoding reversible, and strict decoding ensures peers hash one representation for each value.

## 2026-09-24: Engine registration and local submission ownership

- Stage: 02.
- Decision: Bind each engine's pipeline methods to a copied module registry through `createEngine(modules)`. Order dependency-ready modules by id. Require one explicit initial phase and provide an ordered genesis hook for shared bank, deck and seat fields.
- Alternatives: Use mutable global registration, choose the first phase alphabetically, or force common state initialization into module extension data.
- Reason: Multiple engines can coexist, module order is deterministic, and genesis remains explicit.
- Decision: Commit a `LocalGame.submit` command and its generated inputs atomically. If automatic resolution fails, preserve the previous committed state and log and make the driver terminal. Ordinary invalid player commands remain recoverable. Expose borrowed state and log views, with explicit snapshots for mutable consumers.
- Alternatives: Retry the source after rolling back, or clone the whole state and log on every simulation access.
- Reason: An external source may already have consumed a secret deck item when it fails. Retrying would silently change its outcome. Borrowed views avoid repeated copies in simulations while retaining the engine's immutable-state contract.
- Consultation: A read-only `claude -p` stage review identified non-JSON inputs accepted before log encoding, uncaught automatic-source errors, invariant checks that threw on missing phases, registration edge cases and the single-kind bounds convergence bug. Focused regressions reproduced and fixed these cases. The review also prompted a guard against rule handlers importing RNG through setup reexports. Atomic rollback remains deliberate; the failed batch is never retried. Local committed values are frozen with already-frozen shared branches skipped, and log/event getters copy their arrays. The codec accepts integer values as documented; engine state uses the stricter safe-integer range.

## 2026-09-24: Base card shortages and deterministic dice reset

- Stage: 03.
- Decision: A year-of-plenty command requests exactly two cards and receives the available portion of each requested resource. It does not substitute other types. This pins the project specification's partial-shortage wording, including when unrequested resources remain available.
- Alternatives: Require two available cards whenever the bank's total allows it, or reject any request the bank cannot fill completely.
- Reason: The plan explicitly allows partial fulfillment when a selected resource is unavailable. The [published base-game almanac](https://www.catan.com/sites/default/files/2024-01/Almanac%20CATAN-3D.pdf) describes choosing two resources from supply; the partial-request behavior here is our interpretation of this project's specification, not a claim about that publication.
- Decision: Balanced dice reset to all 36 ordered combinations when the next roll is requested and six or fewer combinations remain. Each external random answer selects uniformly among remaining combinations.
- Reason: This fixes the reset point in the input log and keeps all live randomness outside the engine.
- Decision: Test the literal figure-eight trail on an abstract graph. Real board fixtures retain their maximum vertex degree of three.
- Reason: Two edge-disjoint loops sharing one vertex require degree four. The abstract fixture tests the required trail behavior without changing hex geometry.
- Decision: Pass `HandlerContext` as the final private-handler callback argument, while keeping the external `Engine.applyPrivate` signature unchanged. Production hooks modify demand before bank shortage allocation.
- Reason: Public and private updates must use identical hook-adjusted costs and production. Without the hook context, a private handler could silently calculate different resource changes.
- Decision: Add ordered module private-invariant callbacks and invoke them from `LocalGame` after each input. Base rules check the bank plus exact hands against the initial 19 cards of each resource.
- Reason: Individual hands can fit their public bounds even when a hidden transfer incorrectly changes resource composition. A private conservation check detects that error while keeping the core independent of base-game quantities.
- Consultation: A read-only `claude -p` review of longest-road search and award transfer found no incorrect result for a legal base-game position. We retained exhaustive edge-unique traversal, allowed repeated vertices and blocked passage through opponent buildings. Tests cover the abstract figure-eight, real-board loops and cuts, holder transfers after cuts, and a 15-road three-hex graph whose longest trail has 14 edges. Geometry is cached by its immutable hex-array identity, outside public state.

## 2026-09-24 — Stage 03: trade lifetime and timeout clarifications

- Decision: Keep at most one open offer per proposer. A new offer replaces that proposer's previous offer with a new id and no responses. The active seat can cancel any offer; another seat can cancel its own counter-offer or withdraw its acceptance of an active-seat offer, recording a decline. An actual unanswered trade response has a `mainSec` deadline; optional proposals and withdrawals do not.
- Reason: Repeated proposals otherwise grew shared state without limit, and a responder could remain committed to an offer after deciding to withdraw. Fresh ids ensure an old acceptance cannot authorize a replacement offer.
- Decision: Resolve a zero-card discard automatically. Timeout discards sort piles once by initial size, then drain each pile as needed, breaking ties in canonical resource order. They do not rebalance after each removed card.
- Reason: The zero discard needs no decision, and the timeout wording admitted two interpretations. These choices make the input sequence and the client's private-hand calculation unambiguous.
- Consultation: The read-only Stage 03 review found no additional high-severity defect after the timeout and public/private dice fixes. Its trade lifetime, withdrawal and missing response-deadline findings were reproduced before fixes. It also confirmed the legal-command omission for hidden victory claims during interrupts, which the concurrent legal-action review had already found. The zero-discard and whole-pile timeout clarifications were adopted with regression tests.

- Decision (regions): Desert Crossing declares six disjoint regions: west (home), east and the four corner islets. The five desert-strip hexes belong to no region. The two sides never touch, so any vertex touches at most one region, and a vertex next to a strip hex takes its region from the side hex.
- Decision (Open Sea): `generateArchipelago` (in `modules/seafaring/archipelago.ts`) is a pure function of the genesis random stream. It rejects and retries whole layouts against `archipelagoProblems`, so the constraints in the rules doc are checked on every board it returns. The home island is the largest and never touches the frame's rim. The pirate starts on the sea hex furthest from land.
- Decision (lobby): `scenariosForSeats` and `scenariosForModules` list only scenarios whose modules are in the engine catalogue, so seafaring scenarios appear once the module is registered.
- Also: `scenarioOfConfig` tells fixed scenarios that share a module set apart by their board.

## 2026-09-28: Module-declared decks and the public draw

- Decision (hook): Modules declare decks through the accumulator hook `decks(config, acc)`, which returns `Record<deckId, { cards: Record<cardType, count>; reveal: 'private' | 'public' }>`. The starting value at every call site is the base `dev` deck. The composed hook first folds `devDeck` into `dev.cards`, so `five-six` keeps overriding only `devDeck`. `decksFor`, `deckCatalogueFor` and the old `devCardCatalogueFor` (now a thin wrapper) live in `modules/catalogue.ts`. Base `initializeState` gives every declared deck its public counter, so a module needs no `initializeState` for its decks. Games with only base or five-six declare exactly `dev`, and their states, logs and hashes are unchanged.
- Decision (private draws): Draws from a non-dev private deck keep `CARD_DEALT`. The base `drawDev` frame and the `cardDealt` handler read an optional `deck` from the frame data, check the card against that deck's catalogue, and leave a turn-flow frame through `finishTurnFlowFrame`, so a draw can sit above a turn-end marker. Events and effects for `dev` are unchanged.
- Decision (public draw, engine contract): The pending is `{ kind: 'random', request: { type: 'draw', deck, public: true, seat, slotId, remaining, ... }, systemType }`. The resolving input is `{ kind: 'system', type: systemType, deck, card, ...echoed }`, with every other request field echoed. `publicDrawInput` builds it, and drivers and the ledger share it. The handler emits the new `deck-card-shown` effect, which resource accounting checks like a slot deal without creating an owned slot.
- Decision (public draw, protocol): The deck operation gains an optional `public: true` (absent for private draws, so their operation ids are unchanged). Every seat unlocks in seat order, the drawer included; the final point is the identity point, decoded by matching the deck's canonical cards. `completeDeckDeal` verifies every unlock, decodes the card, and requires the certified input to equal `publicDrawInput(pending, card)`, so a wrong card or altered echoed field is refused before the engine input is accepted. No hidden slot is kept for a public card, but the deck cursor advances. The ledger also requires a request's `public` flag to equal the deck's declared `reveal`, and a public request may not use `CARD_DEALT`. Replay, audit and recovery verify the reveal through the same certified entry.
- Decision (fairness): No seed decides a public draw. The order is the composition of every seat's per-deck shuffle and lock secrets, which derive from each seat's master, not from the genesis seed. The acceptance test draws a whole six-tile deck twice with the same genesis and different secrets and gets different orders; identical secrets reproduce the order. Local and simulation sources deal a hidden stack per deck from their own seed, derived separately from the board seed. The browser source picks uniformly from the undealt cards and keeps them in memory, so a saved local game resumed later restarts a public deck's bookkeeping.
- Decision (ceremony): Genesis creates one deck definition per declared deck in ascending id order, each with its own passes and transcript. Ceremony slot steps are global (`deckIndex × 2 × seats + pass`), so a single-deck game sends identical packets. The step limit is 255, the future-packet buffer and the phase deadline scale with the deck count and total cards, and every deck's passes must be certified in-game before any input. Unlock lists may hold six entries, because a public draw in a six-seat game has six unlockers. The `cheat-capture` size-bound test therefore uses seven unlocks instead of six.
- Accepted limit: The last unlocker of a public draw sees the card before publishing its unlock and could withhold it. Timeouts and takeover handle a silent seat as for any other frozen operation. A colluding set of all seats could predict the order, as with private decks.
- Test seam: `registerAdHocModule` (engine) makes a test module selectable by genesis config without adding it to `MODULE_CATALOGUE`. The synthetic module in `packages/protocol/src/testing/synthetic-decks.ts` declares a public `fog` deck and a private `loot` deck and draws from both between turns.
- Open (return to bottom): Knights returns played progress cards under their deck. The ledger assumes each deck's public `remaining` only falls, so return them in module state instead: a played progress card is revealed publicly, so keep a public queue in the module's `ext` and deal from it, without any deck ceremony, once the deck's hidden positions are used up. If a returned card ever had to be hidden again, a certified re-creation of that deck at the next `deckEpoch` (the definition already supports certified creation) would add it without changing the cursor rule.
- Open (private non-dev reveal): `revealDeckCards` still maps only `PLAY_DEV_CARD` and `CLAIM_VICTORY` to hidden slots. The knights module's play commands must be added there.

## 2026-09-28: Seafaring fog reveals and the archipelago layout

- Decision (layout): `layout: 'fixed' | 'archipelago'` is a seafaring option. With `archipelago` and no `config.board`, `buildBoard` calls `generateArchipelagoLayout(rng, archipelagoParamsFor(seats))` and the pirate starts on the hex the generator names, recomputed with `pirateStartHex` in `initializeState` because `initState` sees no board. Base builds its default board first from the same random stream, so `modifyConfig` leaves `mapLayout` `random` for a generated board (a `standard-fixed` layout with no board is an error in base) and forces `standard-fixed` only for an explicit board. The alternative of adding a board-generator hook to base was rejected: only this module builds boards, and `buildBoard` already runs in module order.
- Decision (decks): Fog is two hidden public decks, `fog-terrain` and `fog-token`, declared by the `decks` hook from `options.fog` with zero counts dropped. Separate stacks follow the publisher's separate terrain and number stacks, and let sea and desert tiles draw no token. Genesis checks that the terrain counts equal the fog hexes and the token counts equal the tiles that take one, so a deck can never run dry mid-game.
- Decision (sequencing): A hex draws its terrain first. Sea and desert finish it. Any other terrain draws a token next, and only that draw changes the hex, so a hex is never a resource terrain without its token and the base invariants hold between the two draws. The drawn terrain is kept in `ext.seafaring.fog.terrain`. Several hexes of one placement finish one after another in ascending hex id order, each with its reward.
- Decision (frame, and the `afterInput` hook): The draws happen in a `fogReveal` frame. A placement command cannot push it itself: base replaces or pops the top frame after `afterBuild` (`advanceSetup`, Road Building's `popPhase`), which would swallow or misplace a frame pushed earlier. So `afterBuild` only queues the hexes in `ext.seafaring.fog`, and a new state hook `afterInput`, called at the end of base's post-input pass while no game result is set, pushes the frame above the phase the command left. It covers setup pieces, Road Building pieces, ordinary builds, special build phases, ship moves and the last setup road (the frame then sits above `preRoll`). The alternative was to override the top phase's pending from the `pending` hook and keep no frame. A trial of it failed: base's hidden-victory auto-claim and `five-six`'s automatic end of the special build phase both look only at the top frame, so they issued commands that the draw pending rejected, and `LocalGame` treats a rejected automatic input as fatal.
- Decision (reward): The revealer takes 1 card of the resource from the bank as soon as the hex is complete, and a bank without it pays nothing. Gold opens a one-card `goldChoice` frame, above the interrupted phase for the last hex and above the fog frame otherwise, so a pending gold choice delays the next draw. The revealer's private hand changes in `applyPrivate` from the same reward function that decided the public transfer.
- Decision (fairness and drivers): Nothing about a tile comes from the genesis seed. Local and simulation play draw from their own seeded stacks, the browser source picks uniformly from the undealt cards, and online play runs the deck ceremony's public draw. All drivers already resolve `random` pendings through `isPublicDraw` and `publicDrawInput`, so none needed to change, and 400 Fogbound games (also with `five-six` and six seats) ran with public and private invariants on. Fog is not a hidden state: the drawn terrain and token are public inputs.
- Decision (resume): Public cards leave no identity in state, so the browser random source could not know which tiles a resumed local game had shown. `BrowserRandomSource.resume(log, state)` rebuilds the shown counts from the replayed input log and throws when the log shows a card more often than the deck holds, or a different number than the state's public draw count. `LocalSession.restore` calls it before going live.
- Test seam: `certifyPublicDraws` in `@cp2p/protocol/testing` runs the real deck ceremony among all-human seats and answers every pending public draw through capture, every unlock, decode, ledger verification and the engine input. The acceptance test (`tools/sim/src/fog-deck-secrets.test.ts`) uses it with the real Fogbound config: one genesis seed and two sets of deck secrets give the same genesis and different tiles.
- Also: `FOGBOUND_FOG` is exported from `@cp2p/maps`. A placement that wins the game leaves its reveal undrawn, because the game has ended. Ship legality next to fog, and a ship moved next to fog, follow the rules doc.

## 2026-09-28: Knights K1 and K2 (commodities, event die, improvements, metropolises)

- Decision (card kinds): The card kinds of a game are the keys of `state.bank`, read by `cardKindsOf(state)` (base resources first, then module kinds by name; base games get the `RESOURCES` constant, so their fast paths and hashes are unchanged). `SeatState.resources` stays typed for the base resources; module kinds ride along as extra keys and are read through `kindBounds` and written through `seatBounds`. Genesis builds the seat bounds and private hands from the `cardKinds` hook. Parsing, exchange, production shortage, steals, discards, timeouts, enumeration and the local driver checks are now kind-generic. `EngineEffect.resource` widened to a string. P2P hand proofs and accounting still cover the base resources only (they reject other kinds); knights over P2P is a later task.
- Decision (event die): `diceSpec` declares an extra die `{ id: 'event', faces: [ship x3, trade, politics, science] }`. The dice request gains an `extra` list (only when a module declares one), and `DICE_RESULT` gains an optional `extra: { event: face }` key (`onDiceResult(state, dice, extra)` receives it). Base games have no `extra` on either, so payloads and goldens are unchanged, and a base game refuses the key. The random derivation labels the dice `red` and `yellow` when a request has extra dice (base keeps `d1`, `d2`) and derives each extra die under its own id (`event`). The derivation checks the request's `extra` against the engine's `diceSpec`, so a forged die list fails. Sources build the faces with `rollExtraDice`. K1 only records `ext.knights.eventDie` and puts `extra` on the `diceRolled` event; K4 and K5 resolve it from `onDiceResult`, which runs before production and the 7 branch, as the rules order requires.
- Decision (robber lock): `robberLike` returns no legal hex and `stealTargets` returns no target while `ext.knights.robberLocked`. Base now skips the robber step after a 7's discards when no hex is legal (`robberStepFrame`), which base games never hit. K4 clears the flag at the first attack.
- Decision (setup city): Round two still uses `PLACE_SETTLEMENT`; knights' `afterBuild` converts the piece to a city (settlement back to supply, city piece used). Base already pays one resource per hex, so the yield is right. The `settlementBuilt` event still says settlement for that placement.
- Decision (hook additions): `bankRate(state, seat, kind, rate)` (accumulator, starts from the harbor rate) lets Trade level 3 make commodity trades 2:1; `onDiceResult` gained the `extra` argument. Other hooks are unchanged.
- Decision (target and dev cards): `vpTarget` is at least 13 (an explicit larger base target is kept). `devDeck` is empty, so `BUY_DEV_CARD` fails with `empty-dev-deck` and no card can be played; Largest Army never triggers.
- Decision (ext state): `ext.knights = { robberLocked, barbarians: { step }, improvements: { trade, politics, science }[] (by seat), metropolises: { trade, politics, science: { seat, vertex } | null }, walls: { seat, vertex }[], eventDie, noProduction }`. `walls` is filled by K3 (the hand limit already reads it); `barbarians.step` by K4. `noProduction` is transient: Aqueduct seats that got no card are noted by `onNoProduction` and consumed by `afterProduction` in the same input.
- Decision (commands): `BUILD_IMPROVEMENT { track }` (main phase and the five-six special build phase, added through the `pending` and `legalCommands` hooks) pays k commodities, priced in the handler because `costOf` does not know the buying seat. `PLACE_METROPOLIS { track, vertex }` answers a `metropolis` frame, pushed only when the seat has several available cities (with one, the metropolis is placed at once). The holder change happens when the city is chosen, so a stolen metropolis moves atomically. `CHOOSE_AQUEDUCT { resource }` answers an `aqueduct` frame holding the queue in turn order from the active seat; the bank is re-read for each seat and an empty resource bank ends the queue. Timeouts: lowest available city, first bank resource.
- Decision (metropolis rules): The level-4 seat takes an unclaimed metropolis; the first seat to level 5 takes it from a holder below level 5; a holder buying its own level 5 keeps it and needs no available city; every other level 4 or 5 purchase needs one. Metropolis points are `stored` public contributions of 2 (city 2 + 2 = 4).
- Decision (bots and sim): `RandomBot` weights `BUILD_IMPROVEMENT`; other new commands are single pending choices. `pnpm sim run --modules base,knights` (or `base,five-six,knights`) runs knights games, and the failure sidecar records `knights`.
- Evidence: Golden replays and all existing tests pass unchanged. Knights tests cover genesis, setup, the production table, per-kind bank shortage, the event die, 7s with the lock, walls and commodities, trading rates, improvements, every metropolis case, the Aqueduct edge cases, five-six with the special build phase, event-die derivation, and random-bot simulations of 2 to 6 seats with public and private invariants on.

- Finding (New Horizons 5-6 players): In the 1,000-game run at six seats, 9 games hit the simulation's 500-turn dead-game cap. Random bots run out of pieces on the full board (some seats hold every settlement, city and ship they own) and then crawl toward the 16-point target. ## 2026-09-28: Fog reveals in the UI and the seafaring fit

- Decision (reveal motion): A fog reveal is a flip. The board already holds the new tile, and a fog sprite over it narrows to an edge, lifts a little, and a light ring glows out and fades (`fogRevealShape`, a pure function with its own test). It takes 1 s. Reveals of one input play one after another, 450 ms apart (`order` on the `fog-reveal` effect, capped at 4 steps), so each tile is seen on its own. Reduced motion skips the effect like every other one, and the existing "Skip animations" button ends it early.
- Decision (revealing indicator): While a `FOG_REVEALED` random pending is open, the next-step bar, the board status pill and the action dock say "Revealing unexplored sea…" with the spinner, and nothing else is offered (the frame's only legal command is a victory claim). A local game resolves the draw inside the same input, so the indicator only ever shows online, where the draw takes the deck ceremony. It is found from the pending (`fogDrawPending`), not from the phase, so a client that has not applied the frame yet still shows it.
- Decision (gold after a reveal): A gold reveal opens its one-card dialog, and the dialog used to open on top of the tile that was turning over. It now waits 1.1 s after the reveal (never with reduced motion), and the status says revealing meanwhile. The choice itself is unchanged and stays forced.
- Decision (log): `fogRevealed` reads "Player revealed forest (6) and drew 1 Lumber.", "... a gold field (5) and may take a card of their choice." or "... revealed sea." The reward is derived from the terrain, so a bank that could not pay is still described as paying (the engine pays nothing then). The event carries no reward, and changing it would touch the engine and its replays for a case that needs a bank without one of 19 cards.
- Decision (bots): Nothing changed. `chooseBotPending` only looks at player pendings, so a random fog draw is left to the driver, and the bot's gold choice after a reveal is an ordinary player pending of the active seat. A test pins both, and the browser test plays every seafaring scenario against bots.
- Decision (fit): A board that lists sea hexes is now fitted to its hexes plus the scenery water ring around them (the ring was cropped before, which read as cut-off top and bottom rows), with 0.1 of a hex extra and 40 px of padding instead of 24, because the phone layout lets the canvas run 30 px past the screen. The fit may go below the pinch floor of 0.35 so a large board still fits whole, and the pinch and wheel floor follows the fit. Base and five-six boards keep their bounds and padding. The tests measure the corners of the hexes and the ring against the canvas and the window.
- Evidence: unit tests for the flip shape, the reveal order, the log lines, the status and the bot choice. `apps/web/tests/seafaring-scenarios.e2e.ts` starts each of the eight seafaring scenarios from the lobby against bots, plays a human seat by legal moves for several rounds, checks the default fit on four screen sizes, and for Fogbound steers the human toward fog until a tile is revealed and checks the state, the log and the effect (and that reduced motion plays none).
- Known gaps: No marker sits on the hex while an online draw is open, only the status line. The log describes the reward from the terrain.
