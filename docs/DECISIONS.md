## 2026-09-24: Clarify milestone A and B contracts before implementation

- Stage: 01, with corrections to the stage 02–04 specifications.
- Decision: Use strict-balance pip caps of 14 for resources on four hexes and 11 for resources on three hexes. Bound generation to 100 terrain retries after each 10,000-attempt token search. Keep ordinary random boards unconstrained and apply balance rules to balanced-random boards.
- Alternatives: A uniform cap of 12, or removing the pip limit. The original uniform cap of 11 is impossible.
- Reason: The tokens total 58 pips, exceeding the original five-resource capacity of 55. The chosen limits account for terrain counts and keep the limit of 11 for three-hex resources. A finite retry bound prevents genesis from hanging.
- Decision: Add `CLAIM_VICTORY { slotIds }` for hidden victory points. Public points win automatically. The local driver submits and records an immediate claim when the active seat's private total wins.
- Alternatives: Store private VP totals in public state, or let public `apply` inspect private state.
- Reason: Both alternatives violate public/private separation or deterministic replay. A claim reveals the winning cards through an explicit input and uses the existing protocol obligation to verify card reveals.
- Decision: Support two-player base games with unchanged rules, as stage 05 already requires. Put board generation under `modules/base/setup`, expose a separate RNG entry for external bots and simulators, and inject enumeration sampling. Clarify local draw ownership, trade-response pendings, offer invalidation, and the validation rules for timeout and seat-status inputs.
- Alternatives: Remove two-player setup, share a hidden RNG with rule handlers, or allow inputs without a defined phase contract.
- Reason: These choices reconcile the existing stage briefs while preserving their required behavior and engine dependency rules.
- Consultation: A read-only `claude -p` review of stages 00–05 confirmed the impossible pip cap and private-VP contradiction and identified the RNG layout, player-count and pending-model inconsistencies. The coordinator checked those findings against the documents. Suggestions to add new acceptance workloads or weaken runtime dependency rules were not adopted.

## 2026-09-24: Resource affordability must preserve a feasible hand

- Stage: 02 specification review, before implementation.
- Decision: Require `max[r] ≥ cost[r]` for every resource and `sum(max(min[r], cost[r])) ≤ total` for public affordability. Use the same guard for known losses, and normalize all bounds updates. Reject infeasible bounds and hidden losses greater than the hand total.
- Alternatives: Check only per-resource maxima, or also check only the total cost.
- Reason: A five-card hand with three guaranteed ore and at most two each of brick and lumber cannot spend two brick plus two lumber. Both alternatives accept that impossible spend. The chosen condition exactly characterizes whether some publicly consistent hand can pay, while preserving the need for later private-hand audits.
- Consultation: A read-only `claude -p` review independently proved the condition by intersecting the hand bounds with the cost lower bounds. It confirmed that the documented loss formulas are sound after this guard and normalization. Stage 02 tests must compare the operations against brute-force feasible hands as well as the required 10k soundness runs.

## 2026-09-24: Canonical byte tag and strict decoding

- Stage: 02.
- Decision: Reserve an exact one-key `{"$b":"..."}` object for `Uint8Array`. Encoding rejects every ordinary one-key object named `$b`, regardless of its value. Decoding accepts that exact shape only when its value is canonical unpadded base64url. `canonicalDecode` also requires re-encoding the decoded value to reproduce the input bytes exactly, rejecting duplicate keys, whitespace, unsorted keys and other noncanonical JSON forms.
- Alternatives: Allow ordinary objects to collide with the byte tag, or accept noncanonical JSON and normalize it during decoding.
- Reason: A single byte tag shape makes snapshot decoding reversible, and strict decoding ensures peers hash one representation for each value.

## 2026-09-24: Engine registration and local submission ownership

- Stage: 02.
- Decision: Bind each engine's pipeline methods to a copied module registry through `createEngine(modules)`. Order dependency-ready modules by id. Require one explicit initial phase and provide an ordered genesis hook for shared bank, deck and seat fields.
- Alternatives: Use mutable global registration, choose the first phase alphabetically, or force common state initialization into module extension data.
- Reason: Multiple engines can coexist, module order is deterministic, and genesis remains explicit.
- Decision: Commit a `LocalGame.submit` command and its generated inputs atomically. If automatic resolution fails, preserve the previous committed state and log and make the driver terminal. Ordinary invalid player commands remain recoverable. Expose borrowed state and log views, with explicit snapshots for mutable consumers.
- Alternatives: Retry the source after rolling back, or clone the whole state and log on every simulation access.
- Reason: An external source may already have consumed a secret deck item when it fails. Retrying would silently change its outcome. Borrowed views avoid repeated copies in simulations while retaining the engine's immutable-state contract.
- Consultation: A read-only `claude -p` stage review identified non-JSON inputs accepted before log encoding, uncaught automatic-source errors, invariant checks that threw on missing phases, registration edge cases and the single-kind bounds convergence bug. Focused regressions reproduced and fixed these cases. The review also prompted a guard against rule handlers importing RNG through setup reexports. Atomic rollback remains deliberate; the failed batch is never retried. Local committed values are frozen with already-frozen shared branches skipped, and log/event getters copy their arrays. The codec accepts integer values as documented; engine state uses the stricter safe-integer range.

## 2026-09-24: Base card shortages and deterministic dice reset

- Stage: 03.
- Decision: A year-of-plenty command requests exactly two cards and receives the available portion of each requested resource. It does not substitute other types. This pins the project specification's partial-shortage wording, including when unrequested resources remain available.
- Alternatives: Require two available cards whenever the bank's total allows it, or reject any request the bank cannot fill completely.
- Reason: The plan explicitly allows partial fulfillment when a selected resource is unavailable. The [published base-game almanac](https://www.catan.com/sites/default/files/2024-01/Almanac%20CATAN-3D.pdf) describes choosing two resources from supply; the partial-request behavior here is our interpretation of this project's specification, not a claim about that publication.
- Decision: Balanced dice reset to all 36 ordered combinations when the next roll is requested and six or fewer combinations remain. Each external random answer selects uniformly among remaining combinations.
- Reason: This fixes the reset point in the input log and keeps all live randomness outside the engine.
- Decision: Test the literal figure-eight trail on an abstract graph. Real board fixtures retain their maximum vertex degree of three.
- Reason: Two edge-disjoint loops sharing one vertex require degree four. The abstract fixture tests the required trail behavior without changing hex geometry.
- Decision: Pass `HandlerContext` as the final private-handler callback argument, while keeping the external `Engine.applyPrivate` signature unchanged. Production hooks modify demand before bank shortage allocation.
- Reason: Public and private updates must use identical hook-adjusted costs and production. Without the hook context, a private handler could silently calculate different resource changes.
- Decision: Add ordered module private-invariant callbacks and invoke them from `LocalGame` after each input. Base rules check the bank plus exact hands against the initial 19 cards of each resource.
- Reason: Individual hands can fit their public bounds even when a hidden transfer incorrectly changes resource composition. A private conservation check detects that error while keeping the core independent of base-game quantities.
- Consultation: A read-only `claude -p` review of longest-road search and award transfer found no incorrect result for a legal base-game position. We retained exhaustive edge-unique traversal, allowed repeated vertices and blocked passage through opponent buildings. Tests cover the abstract figure-eight, real-board loops and cuts, holder transfers after cuts, and a 15-road three-hex graph whose longest trail has 14 edges. Geometry is cached by its immutable hex-array identity, outside public state.

## 2026-09-24 — Stage 03: trade lifetime and timeout clarifications

- Decision: Keep at most one open offer per proposer. A new offer replaces that proposer's previous offer with a new id and no responses. The active seat can cancel any offer; another seat can cancel its own counter-offer or withdraw its acceptance of an active-seat offer, recording a decline. An actual unanswered trade response has a `mainSec` deadline; optional proposals and withdrawals do not.
- Reason: Repeated proposals otherwise grew shared state without limit, and a responder could remain committed to an offer after deciding to withdraw. Fresh ids ensure an old acceptance cannot authorize a replacement offer.
- Decision: Resolve a zero-card discard automatically. Timeout discards sort piles once by initial size, then drain each pile as needed, breaking ties in canonical resource order. They do not rebalance after each removed card.
- Reason: The zero discard needs no decision, and the timeout wording admitted two interpretations. These choices make the input sequence and the client's private-hand calculation unambiguous.
- Consultation: The read-only Stage 03 review found no additional high-severity defect after the timeout and public/private dice fixes. Its trade lifetime, withdrawal and missing response-deadline findings were reproduced before fixes. It also confirmed the legal-command omission for hidden victory claims during interrupts, which the concurrent legal-action review had already found. The zero-discard and whole-pile timeout clarifications were adopted with regression tests.

The home island is the largest and never touches the frame's rim. The pirate starts on the sea hex furthest from land.
- Decision (lobby): `scenariosForSeats` and `scenariosForModules` list only scenarios whose modules are in the engine catalogue, so seafaring scenarios appear once the module is registered.

## 2026-09-28: Module-declared decks and the public draw

- Decision (hook): Modules declare decks through the accumulator hook `decks(config, acc)`, which returns `Record<deckId, { cards: Record<cardType, count>; reveal: 'private' | 'public' }>`. The starting value at every call site is the base `dev` deck. The composed hook first folds `devDeck` into `dev.cards`, so `five-six` keeps overriding only `devDeck`. `decksFor`, `deckCatalogueFor` and the old `devCardCatalogueFor` (now a thin wrapper) live in `modules/catalogue.ts`. Base `initializeState` gives every declared deck its public counter, so a module needs no `initializeState` for its decks. Games with only base or five-six declare exactly `dev`, and their states, logs and hashes are unchanged.
- Decision (private draws): Draws from a non-dev private deck keep `CARD_DEALT`. The base `drawDev` frame and the `cardDealt` handler read an optional `deck` from the frame data, check the card against that deck's catalogue, and leave a turn-flow frame through `finishTurnFlowFrame`, so a draw can sit above a turn-end marker. Events and effects for `dev` are unchanged.
- Decision (public draw, engine contract): The pending is `{ kind: 'random', request: { type: 'draw', deck, public: true, seat, slotId, remaining, ... }, systemType }`. The resolving input is `{ kind: 'system', type: systemType, deck, card, ...echoed }`, with every other request field echoed. `publicDrawInput` builds it, and drivers and the ledger share it. The handler emits the new `deck-card-shown` effect, which resource accounting checks like a slot deal without creating an owned slot.
- Decision (public draw, protocol): The deck operation gains an optional `public: true` (absent for private draws, so their operation ids are unchanged). Every seat unlocks in seat order, the drawer included; the final point is the identity point, decoded by matching the deck's canonical cards. `completeDeckDeal` verifies every unlock, decodes the card, and requires the certified input to equal `publicDrawInput(pending, card)`, so a wrong card or altered echoed field is refused before the engine input is accepted. No hidden slot is kept for a public card, but the deck cursor advances. The ledger also requires a request's `public` flag to equal the deck's declared `reveal`, and a public request may not use `CARD_DEALT`. Replay, audit and recovery verify the reveal through the same certified entry.
- Decision (fairness): No seed decides a public draw. The order is the composition of every seat's per-deck shuffle and lock secrets, which derive from each seat's master, not from the genesis seed. The acceptance test draws a whole six-tile deck twice with the same genesis and different secrets and gets different orders; identical secrets reproduce the order. Local and simulation sources deal a hidden stack per deck from their own seed, derived separately from the board seed. The browser source picks uniformly from the undealt cards and keeps them in memory, so a saved local game resumed later restarts a public deck's bookkeeping.
- Decision (ceremony): Genesis creates one deck definition per declared deck in ascending id order, each with its own passes and transcript. Ceremony slot steps are global (`deckIndex × 2 × seats + pass`), so a single-deck game sends identical packets. The step limit is 255, the future-packet buffer and the phase deadline scale with the deck count and total cards, and every deck's passes must be certified in-game before any input. Unlock lists may hold six entries, because a public draw in a six-seat game has six unlockers. The `cheat-capture` size-bound test therefore uses seven unlocks instead of six.
- Accepted limit: The last unlocker of a public draw sees the card before publishing its unlock and could withhold it. Timeouts and takeover handle a silent seat as for any other frozen operation. A colluding set of all seats could predict the order, as with private decks.
- Test seam: `registerAdHocModule` (engine) makes a test module selectable by genesis config without adding it to `MODULE_CATALOGUE`. The synthetic module in `packages/protocol/src/testing/synthetic-decks.ts` declares a public `fog` deck and a private `loot` deck and draws from both between turns.
- Open (return to bottom): Knights returns played progress cards under their deck. The ledger assumes each deck's public `remaining` only falls, so return them in module state instead: a played progress card is revealed publicly, so keep a public queue in the module's `ext` and deal from it, without any deck ceremony, once the deck's hidden positions are used up. If a returned card ever had to be hidden again, a certified re-creation of that deck at the next `deckEpoch` (the definition already supports certified creation) would add it without changing the cursor rule.
- Open (private non-dev reveal): `revealDeckCards` still maps only `PLAY_DEV_CARD` and `CLAIM_VICTORY` to hidden slots. The knights module's play commands must be added there.

## 2026-09-28: Seafaring fog reveals and the archipelago layout

- Decision (layout): `layout: 'fixed' | 'archipelago'` is a seafaring option. With `archipelago` and no `config.board`, `buildBoard` calls `generateArchipelagoLayout(rng, archipelagoParamsFor(seats))` and the pirate starts on the hex the generator names, recomputed with `pirateStartHex` in `initializeState` because `initState` sees no board. Base builds its default board first from the same random stream, so `modifyConfig` leaves `mapLayout` `random` for a generated board (a `standard-fixed` layout with no board is an error in base) and forces `standard-fixed` only for an explicit board. The alternative of adding a board-generator hook to base was rejected: only this module builds boards, and `buildBoard` already runs in module order.
- Decision (decks): Fog is two hidden public decks, `fog-terrain` and `fog-token`, declared by the `decks` hook from `options.fog` with zero counts dropped. Separate stacks follow the publisher's separate terrain and number stacks, and let sea and desert tiles draw no token. Genesis checks that the terrain counts equal the fog hexes and the token counts equal the tiles that take one, so a deck can never run dry mid-game.
- Decision (sequencing): A hex draws its terrain first. Sea and desert finish it. Any other terrain draws a token next, and only that draw changes the hex, so a hex is never a resource terrain without its token and the base invariants hold between the two draws. The drawn terrain is kept in `ext.seafaring.fog.terrain`. Several hexes of one placement finish one after another in ascending hex id order, each with its reward.
- Decision (frame, and the `afterInput` hook): The draws happen in a `fogReveal` frame. A placement command cannot push it itself: base replaces or pops the top frame after `afterBuild` (`advanceSetup`, Road Building's `popPhase`), which would swallow or misplace a frame pushed earlier. So `afterBuild` only queues the hexes in `ext.seafaring.fog`, and a new state hook `afterInput`, called at the end of base's post-input pass while no game result is set, pushes the frame above the phase the command left. It covers setup pieces, Road Building pieces, ordinary builds, special build phases, ship moves and the last setup road (the frame then sits above `preRoll`). The alternative was to override the top phase's pending from the `pending` hook and keep no frame. A trial of it failed: base's hidden-victory auto-claim and `five-six`'s automatic end of the special build phase both look only at the top frame, so they issued commands that the draw pending rejected, and `LocalGame` treats a rejected automatic input as fatal.
- Decision (reward): The revealer takes 1 card of the resource from the bank as soon as the hex is complete, and a bank without it pays nothing. Gold opens a one-card `goldChoice` frame, above the interrupted phase for the last hex and above the fog frame otherwise, so a pending gold choice delays the next draw. The revealer's private hand changes in `applyPrivate` from the same reward function that decided the public transfer.
- Decision (fairness and drivers): Nothing about a tile comes from the genesis seed. Local and simulation play draw from their own seeded stacks, the browser source picks uniformly from the undealt cards, and online play runs the deck ceremony's public draw. All drivers already resolve `random` pendings through `isPublicDraw` and `publicDrawInput`, so none needed to change, and 400 Fogbound games (also with `five-six` and six seats) ran with public and private invariants on. Fog is not a hidden state: the drawn terrain and token are public inputs.
- Decision (resume): Public cards leave no identity in state, so the browser random source could not know which tiles a resumed local game had shown. `BrowserRandomSource.resume(log, state)` rebuilds the shown counts from the replayed input log and throws when the log shows a card more often than the deck holds, or a different number than the state's public draw count. `LocalSession.restore` calls it before going live.
- Test seam: `certifyPublicDraws` in `@cp2p/protocol/testing` runs the real deck ceremony among all-human seats and answers every pending public draw through capture, every unlock, decode, ledger verification and the engine input. The acceptance test (`tools/sim/src/fog-deck-secrets.test.ts`) uses it with the real Fogbound config: one genesis seed and two sets of deck secrets give the same genesis and different tiles.
- Also: `FOGBOUND_FOG` is exported from `@cp2p/maps`. A placement that wins the game leaves its reveal undrawn, because the game has ended. Ship legality next to fog, and a ship moved next to fog, follow the rules doc.

## 2026-09-28: Knights K3 and K4 (knights, city walls, barbarians)

- Decision (state): `ext.knights` gains `knights: { seat, vertex, level (1 to 3), active, ready, promotedTurn }[]` (ordered by vertex id), `sideways: { seat, vertex }[]`, `defenders: number[]` (Defender of Catan cards per seat) and `lastAttack: { turn, strength, defense, contributions, outcome, defender, tied, pillaged } | null`. `walls` and `barbarians.step` are now filled. Piece supplies are derived (two knights of each level per seat, minus those on the board, minus one waiting in a `displaced` frame), so `piecesLeft` has no knight keys.
- Decision (commands): `BUILD_KNIGHT { vertex }`, `ACTIVATE_KNIGHT { vertex }`, `PROMOTE_KNIGHT { vertex }`, `BUILD_CITY_WALL { vertex }` and `UPGRADE_SIDEWAYS_CITY { vertex }` work in the main phase and in a five-six special build phase. `MOVE_KNIGHT { from, to }`, `DISPLACE_KNIGHT { from, to }` and `CHASE_ROBBER { vertex }` are main phase only (in a special build phase they are not in the `pending` allow-list, so they fail with `not-pending`). `RELOCATE_KNIGHT { to }` answers the `displaced` frame and `CHOOSE_PILLAGE { vertex }` the `pillage` frame. Prices come from the `costs` hook (`knight`, `promote`, `activate`, `cityWall`) so `costOf` can adjust them later (Engineer, Smith, Warlord).
- Decision (readiness): `ready` is set for a seat's active knights in `onTurnStart` (and cleared for every other seat's), so a knight is ready exactly when it was active at the start of its owner's turn and has not acted. Acting sets `active` and `ready` false, activation sets only `active`, promotion keeps both. A knight activated in a special build phase is therefore ready on its owner's next turn, as the rules doc requires. A K5 Deserter knight sets `ready` itself. `promotedTurn` stores `turn.number`, so "once per knight per turn" holds per turn and a special build phase (which keeps the ended turn's number) counts as its own turn for other seats.
- Decision (movement): `knightReach` walks the owner's roads from the knight, passing the owner's buildings and knights, never entering another seat's building, and stopping at another seat's knight (a displacement target only). A road built this turn counts at once. A displaced knight leaves the board into a `displaced` frame (owner out of turn, data carries its level and state) while the displacer already stands on the vertex; the owner's spots are computed from that vertex, which is why the vertex the displacer left can be one of them. With no spot the knight is removed at once and no frame opens. `TIMEOUT` places it on the lowest vertex id.
- Decision (roads and sites): `routeGraph` adds every knight of other seats to `blocked`, and every knight change recomputes Longest Road. The `placement.road` hook refuses a road whose only link is an opposing knight's vertex (a road may still end there), and `placement.settlement` refuses any knight vertex, the owner's included. `placement.city` refuses `BUILD_CITY` while the seat has a sideways piece.
- Decision (barbarians): a ship face in `onDiceResult` moves `barbarians.step`; the seventh resolves the attack there, before production and before a 7, as the rules order requires. Strength is the `city` buildings (a sideways piece is a `settlement`, so it is not counted; a metropolis city counts once). Defense and contributions come from active knights. The best strict contributor gets a Defender point (public, `stored` in `victoryPoints`). A tie at the top is recorded in `lastAttack.tied` and handed to `awardTieDraws(state, seats)`, which does nothing until K5. Pillage picks eligible seats (a city without a metropolis), the lowest contribution among them, and every tied seat loses one city; a seat with exactly one candidate loses it at once. After any attack every knight is inactive and not ready, the ship is back at 0 and the robber is unlocked.
- Decision (held-back roll): a pillage that needs a seat's choice pushes a `pillage` frame over the `dice` frame. ## 2026-09-29: Verified P2P hand machinery over the game's card kinds (knights over the wire)

- Decision (kind list): A game's hand kinds are `kindsOfCounts(state.bank)` (helper `cardKindsFor(config)` in the engine for code that has only a config): the five base resources in today's order, then module kinds by name (knights: `cloth`, `coin`, `paper`, eight in all, `MAX_CARD_KINDS`). Protocol code takes the kinds from the public state it already holds (`before.bank`, `state.bank`) or, inside a steal, from the keys of the frozen victim commitments; nothing new is signed or carried in genesis, because the bank keys are a pure function of the config. Every hand API gained a trailing `kinds` parameter defaulting to the base five (`BASE_CARD_KINDS`), so base, five-six and seafaring games commit exactly the five kinds in the old order and every existing test, golden and wire value is unchanged.
- Decision (commitments and proofs): `SeatHandCommitments.commitments` is a `KindMap<string>` (the five base keys plus one key per module kind), validated against the exact kind list (`kindRecordSchema`), so a wrong, missing or extra key is refused. `planHandTransition` derives obligations per kind (range proofs for gross commodity debits above the public minimum, Schnorr count openings for reveals) and carries `plan.kinds`; the proof `resource` field is a bounded string checked against the derived obligation, so a proof cannot be relabelled to another kind. The six-bit range bound per kind is unchanged. `verifyResourceAccounting` reads kinds from the bank and passes them to every bounds operation (the bounds helpers silently ignore extra kinds when called without them, so every protocol call site now passes `kinds`).
- Decision (hidden steals): `hidden-transfer-v1` already took up to eight kinds, so the one-hot steal proof, the sum proof and the CDS index proof cover eight commitments unchanged. The sealed opening is `{ type, blindings[n] }`, so its length depends only on the public kind count (`stealOpeningBytes(n)`: 5 for base games, `STEAL_OPENING_BYTES` unchanged; 8 for knights) and never on which kind was stolen. `StealOperation.commitments` names every base resource plus at most three well-formed module kind names; the operation must still equal the one rebuilt from the certified hands, so the kind set cannot be chosen by a peer. A contribution whose transfer vector or ciphertext length does not match the kind count is refused (`steal-contribution-shape`). Steal-result folding, the private blinding update and the audit's omniscient index check all walk the same kind list.
- Decision (rest of the wire): Monopoly count operations and inputs name a kind (`kindNameSchema`) that must be one of the game's kinds, which is what K5's commodity and resource monopolies need. Trade-proof planning parses the certified offers over the game's kinds (a five-key strict schema refused every commodity offer). The timed discard, the verified driver's owned-hand checks and blindings, the stub simulation driver and the audit fixture are kind-generic.
- Decision (empty decks): A module that declares a deck with no cards has nothing to shuffle, lock or deal, so genesis deck ceremonies cover only decks with at least one card (`ceremonyDeckIds(config)`). Knights declares its development deck empty until K5 adds progress decks; its games carry no deck commitments, the online ceremony passes zero deck transcripts, and the ledger and genesis checks compare only ceremony decks with the engine's decks. - Decision (opening memo): The verified driver re-checked every owned hand opening after every entry (one Pedersen commitment per kind, three times per entry), which made an eight-kind game audit take about a minute of blocking work. It now skips a seat whose (ledger row, counts, blindings) triple is unchanged since its last verified opening. The check is a pure function of that triple, so this is only a speed-up (a 1,000-entry knights audit went from 57 s to 15 s); a changed row, count or blinding is re-verified in full.

## 2026-09-29: Knights progress cards over the verified P2P protocol

- Decision (seat-signed answers): A request only one seat can answer (a `reveal` pending: `REVEAL_PROGRESS`, `DEAL_KNOWN`, `SHOW_HAND`, `TAKE_CARDS`, `TAKE_PROGRESS`) is answered with a signed command `{ type: 'SEAT_INPUT', input }`, where `input` is the system input without its `kind` and must carry the signer's own seat. `bodyInput(seat, command)` turns it back into the system input everywhere the log derives an input (admission, entry validation, the sequencer's candidate, private replay), so the engine and the audit see the input a local game records, and the nonce, parent binding, signature, proof envelope, cheat findings and timers of a command apply unchanged. A malformed envelope stays an unknown command, which the engine refuses. The seat's own client answers its requests automatically (`VerifiedSessionDriver.revealAnswer`, submitted by `P2PSession`), bots and recovered seats included. Choices (which cards a Master Merchant or Spy takes) are a pure function of the seat's secret and the parent, so a retry repeats them; a UI for choosing is left to the UI.
- Decision (reveals come from effects): `planHandTransition` derives `reveals` (each `card-slot-revealed` effect whose slot has no public `known` identity), `denials` and `moves` from the engine's own effects, so a command cannot pick which slots it proves. `PLAY_PROGRESS_CARD`, each `DISCARD_PROGRESS` entry and a shown victory card are proven with the deck DLEQ against the lock the slot's holder owns; `revealDeckCards` takes the plan's reveals instead of switching on the command type (`PLAY_DEV_CARD` and `CLAIM_VICTORY` map the same way). Accounting learned `card-slot-known` (a slot with `known`, no deck position) and `card-slot-moved`.
- Decision (a hidden Printer or Constitution cannot be denied): `REVEAL_PROGRESS { card: 'none' }` carries a DLEQ OR proof (`@cp2p/crypto` `proveDleqOr`, Cramer-Damgard-Schoenmakers over Chaum-Pedersen) that the drawer's locked point `Z` opens to some identity of the deck that is not a victory card, that is one of the 17 other physical cards, against the public lock key `B`. It reveals nothing about which card, and a drawer holding a victory card has no true branch, so it can only show the card or stall. This was the simplest sound option: it reuses the deck's existing points, adds no ceremony and costs 17 branches. The proof binds the whole signed command and the slot's point.
- Decision (Spy): a private look at another seat's cards is a public-verified card transfer, not a sealed message. The actor's play carries, for each hidden card of the target (locked by the target alone), a fresh lock key `C = c*G` and `c*Z` with a DLEQ. The target answers `SHOW_HAND` with `Z' = b^-1 * (c*Z)`, a DLEQ against its own public lock key: it removed exactly its layer. The actor now holds `c*P`, opens the identity itself, and its `TAKE_PROGRESS` re-locks the taken slot to the ledger (`LedgerSlot.relock`: holder, point and lock key), so the card is later revealed with a DLEQ against `C`. Every scalar `c` is derived from the actor's master, so a restart or replay finds it. Nothing secret crosses the wire, no receipt is needed, and each step is checked by every peer on its move. The open look lives in `DeckLedger.spy` (absent otherwise, so other games hash the same).
- Decision (Master Merchant): the target's `SHOW_HAND` seals the exact opening of its hand (a count byte and a blinding per kind, constant length) to the actor with the same sealed-box the steal uses. The actor checks it against the public commitments and proves the target's debit for its `TAKE_CARDS` itself, since it now knows what those proofs need. The taken kinds are public. A bad seal is only visible to the actor (it cannot make the proofs and stalls); publicly checkable delivery of a blinding needs the steal's receipt phase, which was not built for this card.
- Decision (Commercial Harbor): the swap is applied when the other seat answers, which would need a range proof from the player who is not the signer. The offer therefore carries a debit pre-proof when public bounds do not vouch for the card (`crypto.preproofs`, only present when non-empty): the player proves `count - offered` is a 6-bit value against its current commitment, and the answer's plan skips exactly that obligation while the commitment is unchanged (`coversDebit`, pruned by `prunePreproofs` on every entry). The statement stays true while the commitment does, so it is sound.
- Decision (explicit kinds only): Wedding, Master Merchant and Harbor move explicit kinds in a verified game (public `resource-transfer`), so the ordinary range proofs cover them. The `'hidden'` variants of the engine are refused by the plan (`hand-steal-unavailable`). Consequence: other seats see which kinds were given, taken or swapped, as they see a trade, though not the hands. A multi-card hidden transfer needs the recipient's blindings delivered under the steal's receipt-and-dispute phase; that was left out and is recorded as not met.
- Decision (monopolies and dice): the count operation carries the frame's optional `limit` (Resource Monopoly 2, Trade Monopoly 1) and the completion check expects `min(count, limit)`. A `mode: 'fixed'` dice request (the Alchemist) derives only the extra dice; the two production dice repeat the game's setting and no index is used.
- Decision (private timeouts and bots): a Wedding gift, a Saboteur discard, a Harbor reply and a surplus progress card are answered by the owner's own client when the timer runs out (`timedPrivateCommand`, the same default the engine's exact-hand timeout would choose), exactly like a timed discard; the engine's own `TIMEOUT` still applies when the hand is exactly known. A hosted or recovered bot answers any off-turn private request through `chooseBotPending`. A recovered seat keeps an open look (`adoptRecovered` copies it).
- Decision (audit and replay): the omniscient audit reads a Spy's taken card from the target's hand, and private replay reuses the driver's certified-entry path (a Spy's knowledge and a Master Merchant's opening are re-derived from the certified evidence and the master).
- Decision (lobby and online): knights and knights-56 scenarios are offered online: the picker's `NOT_ONLINE` set is empty, and the online configuration treats a knights scenario like a seafaring one (it brings its modules and options, keeps the generated map choice, and follows the seat count into the five-six scenario). The K6 note that online stays gated no longer holds.
- Evidence: unit tests for the DLEQ OR proof and the debit pre-proof; a four-seat knights game with every one of the 23 progress cards played (chased over further games where the dealing left one out), Spy, Master Merchant, Wedding, Saboteur, Harbor, monopolies, Bishop, Deserter and the victory checks, ended in victory and audited clean through the real network (`knights-progress-game.test.ts`); a cheater suite on the certified entries of that game (forged Spy request and unlock, wrong Master Merchant proof, Wedding gifts of a card never held or of the wrong size, a concealed victory card, swapped deck proofs), each refused on its move with the signer as the certified offender.
- Evidence (chaos and limits): the nine stage-06 chaos scenarios ran one at a time on a verified knights four-seat game (`docs/verification/knights-p2p/`), all 1/1 with complete audits. The network driver now also picks a choice owed off turn, and the excluded seat's stand-in actor answers requests only that seat can answer (`revealCommand`). ## 2026-09-29: Trading with hosted bots

- Problem (reported from a local game against bots): accepting a bot's offer often did nothing. A bot that offered chose its next move at random. It rarely picked `CONFIRM_TRADE` and often ended its turn, which cleared the offer without a word. The bot scheduler (`chooseBotPending`) stopped at the first seat that owed a reply, so while a person had not answered, the bots after them in seat order waited as well. Bots also accepted 3 in 10 offers at random, including ones they could not pay for, and ignored counter-offers made to them.
- Decision (settling): Hosted bots, local and online, now decide through `decideHosted` (`@cp2p/bots`). Before its own policy, a bot settles trades. On its turn it trades its open offer with the first seat (lowest seat number) that accepted and can pay, or withdraws the offer when nobody did. It takes a counter-offer made to it if it wants the trade, and otherwise turns it down with `CANCEL_TRADE`. It answers an offer on its merits (`wantsTrade`): it must hold the cards and keep what its next build needs, and it then accepts a trade that brings a card that build lacks, or that gives it at least as many cards as it pays.
- Decision (waiting): `chooseBotPending` lets hosted bots reply at once, even when a seat earlier in order still owes a reply. When only replies from people (or from bots hosted elsewhere) remain, it picks the offering bot anyway, and `botAwaitsTradeReplies` tells the scheduler to wait `BOT_TRADE_PATIENCE_MS` (15 s) instead of the normal bot delay. Any reply is a new log entry and restarts the timer, so the bot settles at its normal pace once the last answer is in. After the wait it settles with whoever has accepted by then. Both schedulers (`LocalSession`, `P2PSession`) use the same rule, and the wait can be configured (`botTradePatienceMs`).
- Decision (unchanged simulation): `RandomBot.decide` is unchanged, and the simulator and golden generators still call it directly. Every base, seafaring and knights golden replay therefore stays byte-identical. Changing the random policy itself would have meant regenerating all of them for a change that only matters when people play.
- Decision (feedback): The offer panel shows one status line: waiting for replies; "X accepted — trade to complete it" (or choose among several); everyone declined; "You accepted. Waiting for X to confirm."; or, for a counter-offer, waiting for the active player. A `TradeNotice` above the offers says how an open trade the viewer was part of ended: completed, traded with someone else, withdrawn, turned down, or expired at the end of the turn. Offers the viewer closed themselves get no notice.
- Evidence: bot tests for replies, confirming with the first acceptor, withdrawing, and taking or refusing counter-offers. Protocol tests for the reply order and the wait. `LocalSession` tests with a fake clock: a bot's offer that a person accepts, one they leave unanswered past the wait, and a person's offer that a bot accepts. Web tests for every status line and notice. `apps/web/tests/bot-trade.e2e.ts` plays both directions in a local game against bots with no page errors.

## 2026-09-29: Hand counts follow the flying cards; steals show the real card to its parties

- Problem: the hand's counts (and a panel's card total) changed the moment the state did, and the production cards then flew to a slot that already showed the new number. A steal showed the thief and the victim the same card back as everyone else.
- Decision (lagging counts): the counts on screen are the true counts less the cards still in the air. Each flight registers a hold (`card-holds.ts`, a small store beside the session store) in the same tick as the state update: a card flying in keeps its receiver's count down until it reaches its slot (the 80% mark of its animation, by timer, so a paused or hidden animation cannot stall it), and a card flying out keeps its giver's count up until it takes off. The hand dock (per kind and the "N cards" total) and the player panel totals read through the holds; dialogs and pickers keep reading the true hand. Holds never change what the rules see.
- Decision (settling): releasing every hold always gives back the state, so the counts cannot drift (a new batch of cards releases the previous ones at once; see "Production starts as the dice settle"). Every hold has a deadline a little past its flight; Skip animations, reduced motion, a session change, unmounting and a jump (an update with no events whose state or known hands changed, or a lower revision: restore, dev replace, resync) drop them all, and reduced motion never creates any. A new board renderer (the phone layout remounts it) only resubscribes and keeps the cards already in the air.
- Decision (which cards fly, and whose kinds show): flights come from the public events (`visual-effects.ts` turns trades, steals, Master Merchant/Spy takes, Wedding gifts, monopoly collections, gold choices and discards into transfers) and from the change in the revealed seat's own hand, which is what the client itself knows locally and online (its private state after STEAL_RESULT or a hidden steal). For a transfer the viewer is a party to, the face is a kind that actually moved in or out of its hand; anything the events do not explain (bank trades, payments, Year of Plenty, the Aqueduct, fog rewards, harvests) flies from or to the bank. For a transfer between other seats the card is a back, unless its kinds are public (player trade terms, monopoly, gold). A private face is drawn only while its own seat's hand is on screen at take-off; otherwise it falls back to a back. `card-flights.ts` holds the per-viewer assignment.
- Decision (end points): a card leaves or lands on its kind's slot in the hand dock (scrolled into view first when it is clipped, on desktop and phone), else the hand, else the seat's panel; a panel scrolled out of the player rail is reached at the rail's nearest edge. The bank end is that kind's bank card, else the bank, else the board. Face-up card flights now last 650 ms (like the card back) so a card can be seen landing (1.15 s since "Production reads slower, from an earlier start").
- Known gap: when several events in one update move the same kinds (a Bishop stealing from two players), the viewer's gained kinds are assigned to the events in kind order, since the hand change does not say which card came from whom. Bank counts are not held back.
- Evidence: unit tests for the per-viewer selection (thief, victim, observer and no viewer, with no kind in an observer's cue), the hold arithmetic, and the hook's timing (held until landing, dropped at take-off, reduced motion, rapid updates, jump, skip, unreachable ends, deadline); `apps/web/tests/card-count-flights.e2e.ts` rolls forced dice against bots on desktop and phone and checks the old counts mid-flight and the new ones after landing; the steal test in `local-game.e2e.ts` checks the stolen kind flies between its hand slot and the other player's panel in both directions.

## 2026-09-29: Production starts as the dice settle; new cards never queue

- Problem: after a roll the dice tumbled (1.4 s), then the producing number tokens grew (1.2 s), and only then did the cards take off (1.4 s flights, 140 ms apart), so a payout finished about 4 s after the roll. With fast bots the next roll arrived while tokens were still expanding, and the cards and held counts of several rolls piled up behind each other. Two barbarian ships could also show at once (a new sail or attack started while the last one ran, and the first one's end revealed the resting ship too).
- Decision (timing, since slowed and moved earlier: see "Production reads slower, from an earlier start"): the dice settle on their final faces at 600 ms (`DICE_SETTLE_MS`; the dice stay readable until they fade at 1.4 s). At that moment the token pulse (now 700 ms) and the production cards start together, from the producing hexes (robber-blocked hexes still pay nothing and fly nothing). Cards leave 70 ms apart, squeezed so a whole payout leaves within 350 ms, and fly for 850 ms, counting as landed at 700 ms; the last card lands at most about 1.05 s after the dice settle. Cards paid without a roll fly at once. The barbarian ship also sets sail as the dice settle.
- Decision (no queueing): an update that rolls or moves cards first fast-forwards whatever is still in the air: the overlays go, every hold is released (so the counts show the state) and queued launches are dropped, then the new cards start. Updates that move no cards (a build, the end of a turn) leave flights alone. On the board only the newest dice, token pulse, robber, pirate and barbarian (sail or attack) effect runs: a new one ends the running one at once, including its clean-up (`effectChannel` in `effectMotion.ts`). Reduced motion and Skip animations stay instant.
- Evidence: unit tests for the launch schedule and the 1.2 s budget, a second roll mid-flight (the first cards land at once, only the new holds remain, the counts converge), a build leaving flights alone, the dice settle pose and the effect channels; `apps/web/tests/production-timing.e2e.ts` measures the first card leaving about 630 ms after the roll while the tokens pulse, and plays a human roll straight into three zero-delay bot turns, checking no dice, pulse, robber or barbarian effect ever runs twice at once and every panel count matches the state as soon as the last payout lands.

## 2026-09-29: Production reads slower, from an earlier start

- Problem: production started at 600 ms with a 700 ms token pulse and 850 ms flights squeezed into 350 ms, which felt rushed; it was tuned for 450 ms bots, and most people play at a slower pace (now 1 s by default, see "Bots act once a second by default").
- Decision (earlier): the dice tumble is shorter and settles at 450 ms (`DICE_SETTLE_MS`; the dice stay readable until they fade at 1.4 s). The producing number tokens start to grow at 300 ms, during the last wobble (`PRODUCTION_PULSE_START_MS`), so nothing waits on the dice; they are at their largest just after the cards take off.
- Decision (slower): the pulse lasts 1.2 s. Production cards take off as the dice settle, 130 ms apart (`PRODUCTION_STAGGER_MS`), squeezed only when a payout has more than four cards so the batch leaves within 450 ms (`PRODUCTION_SPREAD_MS`). Each card lifts off its hex (a 140 ms pop), glides with an ease-out and reaches its slot at 80% of a 1.15 s flight (`CARD_LANDS_MS` = 920 ms, when the count ticks), rests and fades. A four-card roll is gone about 2 s after the roll: before a bot at the default pace can roll again (it ends its turn a second after rolling, and the next bot rolls a second later). Trade, steal and other card flights use the same 1.15 s flight and 80% landing, and the barbarian ship sails for 1.15 s from the dice settling.
- Unchanged: new cards never queue (a roll or card movement still fast-forwards the cards in the air, the safety net for fast bots), one effect per board channel, reduced motion and Skip animations stay instant.
- Evidence: unit tests for the pulse window, the dice settle pose, the launch schedule (130 ms beat, 450 ms cap, every count within 1.4 s of the settle, a four-card roll finished within two default bot delays), the landing times and hold deadlines; `production-timing.e2e.ts` measures the first card leaving about 480 ms after the roll and the last gone within 2.3 s, keeps the rapid zero-delay bot check, and adds a run of three bots at the default pace where no roll arrives while the previous roll's cards are still in the air. Frames of a roll were inspected (tokens growing under the settling dice, cards lifting off, gliding in).

## 2026-09-29: Bots act once a second by default

- Problem: new local games defaulted to a bot pace of 450 ms (online hosted bots 800 ms, the protocol's own fallback 350 ms, an imported game without presentation 500 ms). At 450 ms a bot's next roll arrived while the last payout was still in the air, so the cards were fast-forwarded most of the time, and most people do not play against bots that fast.
- Decision: one default, `DEFAULT_BOT_DELAY_MS` = 1000 ms (exported by `@cp2p/protocol`), for the new local game form, online hosted bots, `P2PSession` when a game gives none, and imported games that carry no presentation. A roll's cards now land before the next bot acts. Saved games keep the pace they were created with (it is stored in their presentation); the form still accepts 0 to 60 000 ms.
- Evidence: `local-game.e2e.ts` checks the form's default; protocol and web unit tests pass with explicit delays as before.

## 2026-09-29: Stage 16 bots — runtime, levels and honesty

- Decision (runtime): a bot decides through a message protocol (`decide` with `{ view, pending, legal?, timeBudgetMs?, iterationBudget? }`, `respondTrade`, `dispose`) handled by a `BotHost` that multiplexes every bot of one bot host, keyed by seat. In the browser the host runs in a dedicated Web Worker (`apps/web/src/session/bot-worker.ts`): local games post to it from the page, and online games from the protocol worker (a nested worker). Tests and environments without workers use the same host inline. In the simulator the bots run inside the existing `worker_threads` batches.
- Decision (honesty): the only view a bot gets is `BotView = { state, priv, seat }`, the public state plus its own seat's `PrivateState`. `createBotView` builds exactly these three fields and `parseBotRequest`/`assertBotView` refuse any message with another field (another seat's hand, a `privates` map), a view whose private state belongs to another seat, or a state or private state with unknown top-level fields. The difficulty-honesty test plays full games with a view in which reading any absent field throws, and a `@ts-expect-error` shows the type has no such field.
- Decision (randomness): each bot has its own RNG. Online, its seed is HKDF of the bot seat's master secret under the new label `bot` (`deriveBotSeed(master, { game, seat })`), so a hosted bot's play can be reproduced from the secret for audits and debugging. Local games draw a fresh 32-byte seed per bot; simulations derive it from the simulation seed. Searching bots take a wall-clock budget in the browser (300 ms per decision) and a fixed iteration budget in simulations, so tournament results are reproducible on any machine.
- Decision (never illegal): every heuristic decision is either one of the seat's legal concrete commands (from `getLegalCommands`) or a command built from a legal template and checked with `engine.validate` before it is returned. A request no policy covers falls back to the random bot for that move and reports it through `DecideContext.warn`; the tournament output counts these warnings per level and command set.

- Decision (asynchronous bots): `P2PSession`'s `decideBot` may now return a promise (a bot in its own worker). An answer that arrives after the log moved on, or while a recovery installs, is dropped; the scheduler asks again for the new head. A synchronous answer behaves exactly as before.
- Decision (humanlike pace and trades): heuristic bots wait the table's pace scaled by the decision's weight (opening settlement ×1.8, robber ×1.5, discard or steal ×1.2, a road ×1.1, rolling ×0.7, with ±25% jitter; Hard ×1.15) and answer another seat's trade offer after 1–3 s. Random bots keep their exact old timing, so existing local tests and saves are unchanged. Normal and Hard propose at most one 1:1 trade per turn (a spare card for the card their goal needs most, when they are at most two cards short), to people and bots alike, and settle it through the existing hosted-bot trade flow (`hostedTradeCommand`), now judged by the bot's own trade evaluation.
- Decision (thinking indicator): a bot seat that owes a move shows "Thinking" with three pulsing dots in the player rail, and every bot seat shows its level. It is derived from the public pending list and the game presentation (which now records each bot seat's level), so it needs no session events and works the same locally and online.

## 2026-09-29: Stage 16 bots — what the levels are

- Easy: greedy. Pip-sum placement, builds whatever VP it can afford first, bank trades toward its goal, never offers trades, accepts a trade that brings a missing card for no more cards than it gives, robs the richest opponent hex. Its settlement plans look at most one road ahead.
- Normal: heuristic. Full vertex score (pips weighted by need, new resources, harbor synergy, expansion room), a resource plan (turns-to-afford per goal, VP per expected turn), trade evaluation (a card's value is its reduction in turns-to-goal; a partner within 2 VP of winning must offer much more), robber targeting weighted by the leader, discards that keep the goal's cards, monopoly by inferred opponent hands, and occasional 1:1 offers to other players.
- Hard: the Normal heuristic with four refinements that measured stronger (development cards whenever affordable, spending down before a seven, roads toward the longest road, knights played early), plus a determinized Monte Carlo search at the opening settlements (see the search entry). Rollouts use the Hard heuristic.
- Module plugins: seafaring (ship expansion to the best ship-reachable site when no land site is near, gold choice by hand value, pirate beside the most threatening opponent ships, road or ship in setup) and knights (improvements with science first, knights built and activated when the barbarians are within three faces or the bot is the weakest defender, walls when holding seven or more cards, progress cards played now and then and always when near the limit, deck, aqueduct, metropolis, pillage and discard choices). Anything else falls back to random with a warning.
- Online levels: the signed lobby field keeps its values `easy | medium | hard`; `medium` plays Normal. The lobby now offers Easy, Normal and Hard bots (it offered one "Random bot" that was `easy`).

## 2026-09-29: Stage 16 bots — the search, and how the levels were tuned

- Decision (search): the Hard bot's search is a determinized Monte Carlo search over the root decision. Each iteration samples one world consistent with the bot's view (opponent hands from their public bounds weighted by their production, unplayed development cards from the cards not yet seen, the deck order), plays every candidate in that world with the same dice stream (a paired comparison, which cuts the noise of Catan's dice), rolls out with the Hard heuristic (8 turns after the robber, 24 after an opening settlement) and scores the leaf as the bot's standing minus the best opponent's (points, production, cards, development cards, harbors). The heuristic's own move is kept unless another is ahead by more than 0.08 points. Chance events the sampler does not model (module decks, the event die) end a rollout, so games with seafaring or knights play the heuristic alone.
- Decision (what is searched): the opening settlements and the robber. Main-phase build choices (a macro-action per build kind, or saving) are implemented but off: at 12 iterations a search of all three decisions over the Normal heuristic won 15% of 40 games against three Normal bots (the heuristic alone: 26%). Searching the opening or the robber alone measured neutral at 8 iterations (41.3% and 40.5% of 600 games against three Normal bots, against about 39–41% for the Hard heuristic alone). The search is kept because the plan requires it and it costs nothing in strength; most of Hard's edge comes from its heuristic.
- Decision (budgets): 300 ms per searched decision on a desktop and 150 ms on a phone or tablet (by user agent). The search stops between rollouts when the budget runs out, drops a partial iteration, and leaves the heuristic's move when not one iteration fits. Simulations use 6 iterations, about what 300 ms buys on a desktop (an opening rollout costs about 10 ms). Only the opening and the robber are searched, so every other decision takes well under a millisecond.
- Decision (tuning): the level configurations were tuned with in-process tournaments of one candidate against three opponents with seat rotation (400–1,200 games per variant, seeds 7–21), then fixed; the acceptance tournaments use fresh seeds (1600–1602). Buying development cards whenever affordable was the largest single gain (about +10 points of win share against Normal), then roads toward the longest road (+2) and spending down before a seven (+1). A simpler robber, extra weight on cities or settlements, diversity, expansion and trade thresholds measured within noise or worse. Easy's settlement plans look one road ahead, not two, which lowers it against Normal and barely changes it against Random (84.6% against 87.5%).
- Decision (resource use, user request): tuning and acceptance runs use at most two processes, one run at a time, and wait while another agent's browser tests run.

## 2026-09-30: Stage 16 follow-up A — the knights policy

- Decision (a frozen benchmark): the stage 16 knights policy is kept unchanged as `plugins/knights-v1.ts`, and the simulator accepts the benchmark levels `hard-v1` and `normal-v1` (`SimBotLevel`, never offered in a game's setup or the lobby). Every change is measured head to head against the policy before it, and the final runs against these frozen levels. Easy keeps the stage 16 policy, so the levels stay apart; the new policy's choices that did not change (decks, the Aqueduct, metropolis and pillage cities, the Deserter, relocation, walls) are read from the frozen file.
- Decision (progress cards): each card has a value of playing it now, in resource cards (a point is about six), and a value of keeping it; a card is played when the first beats the second, or nearly any useful play once three or four cards are held (a new draw over four forces a discard). Cards that depend on hidden hands use only public counts and bounds (Saboteur and Wedding by public hand sizes, monopolies by the expected counts from the bounds, Spy by public progress counts). Crane goes with an improvement purchase, Medicine with a city, the Alchemist before the roll on the total that pays the bot most (never a 7, the red die low when the bot's improvements draw at least as well as anyone's). Over the limit the bot plays what still helps, then discards the card least worth keeping.
- Decision (barbarians): the chance of an attack before the bot's next action phase follows from the track (each roll shows a ship half the time). The bot aims for the cheaper of out-ranking the weakest eligible seat (assuming every opponent activates all its knights) or the island holding, plus the Defender point when that costs one more strength. It recruits when the attack is at least 30% likely within three rounds and activates within two, before the turn's builds, and values grain for the activation in trades and discards. Keeping wool and ore for a recruit as well cut lost cities (0.83 against 1.09 a game) but cost more cities and improvements than it saved, so only grain is kept.
- Decision (metropolis race): improvements are valued by what they win: level 4 on an unclaimed track or level 5 over a level-4 holder is worth two points and is bought before anything else in the turn, level 3 adds its ability (the Aqueduct most). When such a purchase is one or two bank trades short, the bot trades the cards it misses least for the commodity. Commodity values per kind in the hand score measured neutral and were not kept.
- Decision (not kept): knight actions for blocking (displacing blockers, parking on contested sites or the leader's road, recruit sites to match) measured 25.3% of 3,200 games against the policy without them, and walls by the expected hand against the limit 26.2%, both within noise of 25%; per the plan ("a change that does not measurably help is not kept") knights keep the stage 16 chase-when-spare rule and walls the stage 16 rule.
- Decision (measurement): tuning ran 400-game batches, two seeds each on `knights` and `four-isles-knights`, at most five processes while the load average stayed below 8 (user decision, 2026-09-30; two otherwise), never beside a browser run; the final 2,000-game comparisons use seeds never used for tuning. The base-game thresholds were rerun on the stage 16 seed to show they reproduce exactly.

## 2026-09-30: The steal sheet — picking a face-down card is cosmetic

- Stage: web follow-up (user request).
- Decision (fairness): the thief may tap one of the victim's face-down cards on a "Steal a card" sheet, but the tap never chooses the card. The card stolen is still drawn by the existing fair steal: the engine's `stealResult` random pending, answered locally by the session's random source and online by the verified P2P steal protocol (contributions and hidden transfer proofs). The tapped index lives only in the web client's `useStealReveal` store for the sheet's animation. It is never part of a command (the sheet submits the engine's own legal `STEAL { victim }` object unchanged), a protocol message, the game state, a save or a log, so neither the victim nor observers can learn it. The tapped back simply turns over to show whatever the fair draw produced. Showing N backs reveals nothing new: the victim's card total is public. The sheet says so ("The card you get is drawn at random, whichever one you pick").
- Alternatives: let the tap pick an index into the victim's hand (the victim would have to order the hand, which leaks information and needs a new hidden-order protocol; rejected), or keep the old victim list only (the user asked for the more visual flow).
- Decision (sequencing): the tap submits `STEAL`; the tapped back pulses until the result reaches this client, then turns over (0.42 s), stays face up briefly, and the sheet closes and the face flies into its hand slot, reusing the card flights and count holds so the count ticks up on landing. Until then the thief's hand count is held with a pinned hold (new flights keep it); the victim's count drops when the result arrives. A result that never comes closes the sheet after 30 s; a submission that fails lets the thief tap again.
- Decision (sources): every steal that asks the thief for a victim (the robber after a 7 or a knight, the pirate) goes through the sheet, straight away when there is one victim. A Bishop robs with no victim choice, so its steals, recognised by the viewer's `progressCardPlayed` for the Bishop, open the sheet one victim after another after the fair results are already in. Any other steal into the viewer's hand that it did not make on the sheet (a turn-timer steal, a scripted submit) just flies.
- Decision (setting): "Pick the card to steal" in Settings, on by default, stored as an optional `pickStealCard` in the version 2 settings record (absent in older records means on, so no migration). Off, the victim choice steals at once and the stolen card leaves the victim's panel face down and turns over in flight. Reduced motion shows the face at once for 0.7 s with no flip and no flight. The result is announced to screen readers ("You stole 1 Ore from Bob") either way.
